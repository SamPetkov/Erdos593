\section{A rank-three entropy problem and restricted density bounds}
\label{sec:rank-three-entropy-research}

This note concerns a possible density extension of the finite atom description,
not a further proof of the obligatory classification.  Cycle and theta atoms
suggest asking whether the next trivalent core, $K_4$, admits an exact
edge-marginal entropy construction.  We obtain restricted density and entropy
statements, including bounds for flat-spectrum and hierarchical hosts, every
weighted three-state host, and a host-dependent mixing criterion.  We also identify constructions that cannot
establish the unrestricted result.
The kernel and entropy arguments below have analytical source review,
not Lean validation.  The finite coefficient comparison has separately
passed a pinned warning-fatal Lean check.  A complete finite kernel-energy
transport candidate is now written, but has not been compiled;
canonical integration remains pending.  Novelty and significance
have not been established.

The initial equal-spoke, one-step compensation argument, the binary path
criterion and the obstruction examples were developed with a returned ChatGPT
Pro response.  The arbitrary-mismatch, unequal-spoke and two-diamond
refinements below were derived and checked separately during its audit.
The displayed model-version identity was unavailable.  These provenance
statements do not supply mathematical validation.

\subsection{The exact target and its boundary}

Replace each edge $ij$ of $K_4$ by a path of length $L_{ij}\geq1$,
with mutually disjoint interiors, and write $J=J(L)$.
The paths count edges, so
\[
 m=\sum_{ij}L_{ij},\qquad n=|V(J)|=m-2,\qquad m-n+1=3.
\]
We require $L_{ij}\equiv\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j\pmod2$ for some
branch colours $\varepsilon_i\in\{0,1\}$; thus $J$ is simple and bipartite.
Let $\eta$ be a symmetric exchangeable probability law on $\Omega^3$, where
$\Omega$ is finite.  Its pair and singleton marginals are $\lambda$ and
$\kappa$.  Restrict $\Omega$ to the positive support of $\kappa$; zero
entries of $\lambda$ remain allowed.  All logarithms are natural.
The question is whether there exists a probability law $\tau$ on the
\emph{actual} vertices of $J$ such that every oriented edge has marginal
$\lambda$ and
\begin{equation}
 D(\tau\Vert\kappa^{V(J)})\leq mD(\lambda\Vert\kappa^2).
 \label{eq:rank3-entropy-target}
\end{equation}
Every vertex is incident with an edge, so the prescribed edge laws fix all
singleton laws.  Hence the same question asks for
$H(\tau)\geq mH(\lambda)-(m+2)H(\kappa)$.
Mere feasibility is easy: use one sample from $\lambda$ on the two
bipartition classes.  It is the entropy budget that needs proof.

Known scalar density coverage must be removed before interpreting examples.  Sidorenko's
small-part theorem already covers $L=(2,3,1,1,1,2)$, which has parts of size
$4$ and $4$.\footnote{A.~Sidorenko, \emph{A correlation inequality for
bipartite graphs}, Graphs and Combinatorics 9 (1993), 201--204,
\url{https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02988307}.}
For $L_e=2a_e$, halving the paths gives a graph with $\sum_ea_e-2$
vertices and common kernel $T^2$.  Sidorenko's doubly-nonnegative theorem
therefore covers $\sum_ea_e\leq7$.\footnote{A.~Sidorenko,
\emph{Inequalities for doubly nonnegative functions}, Theorem 7.1 and
the subdivision discussion, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08210}.}
Neither fact proves the target for arbitrary lengths and pair laws.

\subsection{A compensation bound with arbitrary arm mismatch}

Let $(\Omega,\mu)$ be a finite \emph{probability} space with
$\mu_x>0$ and $\sum_x\mu_x=1$.  Let $W$ be a symmetric kernel with
$0\leq W\leq1$ and constant row mean $p>0$:
\[
 \sum_y\mu_yW(x,y)=p.
\]
Kernel composition and inner products use $\mu$.  Put
$T=W/p$, $A=T^2$, $\Pi f=\int f\,d\mu$,
$K_j=A^j$, and $B_j=A^j-\Pi$ for $j\geq1$.
The self-adjoint Markov contraction $T$ fixes constants.  On the orthogonal
complement of constants, $A$ has eigenvalues $\theta_i\in[0,1]$.
For an orthonormal eigenbasis $\phi_0=1,\phi_1,\ldots$, write
\[
 B_j(x,y)=\sum_{i\geq1}\theta_i^j\phi_i(x)\phi_i(y),
 \qquad M_{ist}=\int\phi_i\phi_s\phi_t\,d\mu.
\]

For six positive integers $a_e$, the normalized density of the even
subdivision is
\[
 F(a)=\frac{t(J(2a),W)}{p^{2\sum_ea_e}}
     =\int_{\Omega^4}\prod_{e=vw\in E(K_4)}K_{a_e}(x_v,x_w)\,d\mu^4.
\]
Expand each factor as $1+B_{a_e}$.  Any term with a degree-one vertex
vanishes.  The remaining terms are the empty term, four triangles,
three four-cycles, six diamonds, and the full tetrahedron:
\begin{equation}
 F=1+\mathcal T+\mathcal C+\sum_eD_e+Q.
 \label{eq:rank3-complete-expansion}
\end{equation}
Here $D_e$ omits edge $e$.  Each triangle or cycle equals
$\sum_i\theta_i^{\sum a_e}\geq0$.
For example,
\[
 D_{cd}=\sum_{i,s,t\geq1}
 \theta_i^{a_{ab}}\theta_s^{a_{ac}+a_{bc}}
 \theta_t^{a_{ad}+a_{bd}}M_{ist}^2\geq0.
\]
Thus only $Q$ can be negative; its sign alone is not a density counterexample.

For the ordered tuple $(k,r+h,u,r,u,\ell)$ considered below, the slices
$k=r,\ u=\ell$; $k=r+h,\ u=\ell$; and $k=u,\ r=\ell$
have equal spokes at a pair of branch vertices.  Splitting their connecting
even path at its midpoint gives two reflected bags with a common even-cycle
core and forest separator.  These are covered by the reflective-decomposition
method, not new universal cases of this note.\footnote{L.~N.~Coregliano
and A.~A.~Razborov, \emph{Biregularity in Sidorenko's Conjecture},
Theorem 3.5 and its symmetric adaptation in Section 8,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06599}.}

\begin{proposition}[A two-diamond compensation bound]
Suppose, in the order $ab,ac,ad,bc,bd,cd$,
\[
 a=(k,r+h,u,r,u,\ell),\qquad
 k\geq u\geq1,\quad r\geq\ell\geq1,\quad h\geq1.
\]
Set $d=r-\ell$ and
\[
 \gamma_{d,h}=\frac14\max_{0\leq x\leq1}
       \frac{x^d(1-x^h)^2}{1+x^h}.
\]
Here $x^0=1$, including at $x=0$.
If $p\geq\gamma_{d,h}$, then $t(J(2a),W)\geq p^m$, where
$m=2k+4r+4u+2h+2\ell$ and $|V(J(2a))|=m-2$.
The scale-invariant condition for bounded regular $W\geq0$ is
$p/\|W\|_\infty\geq\gamma_{d,h}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $z,w$, set
\[
 f(x)=B_{r+h}(x,z)B_u(x,w),\qquad
 g(x)=B_r(x,z)B_u(x,w),\qquad
 H=(f+g)/2,\quad\delta=(f-g)/2.
\]
Self-adjointness gives
\[
 Q+D_{cd}=\int K_\ell(z,w)\langle f,B_kg\rangle\,d\mu(z)d\mu(w)
 =\int K_\ell(z,w)
   \bigl(\langle H,B_kH\rangle-\langle\delta,B_k\delta\rangle\bigr)
   \,d\mu(z)d\mu(w).
\]
Since $K_\ell\geq0$ and $B_k\succeq0$, this is at least $-E$, where
$E=\int K_\ell\langle\delta,B_k\delta\rangle\,d\mu^2\geq0$.
Every positive power of $T$ is obtained by Markov averaging, so
$K_\ell=T^{2\ell}\leq1/p$ pointwise.  It follows that
$E\leq E_0/p$, with the exact expression
\[
 E_0=\frac14\sum_{i,s,t\geq1}
 \theta_i^k\theta_s^{2r}(1-\theta_s^h)^2
 \theta_t^{2u}M_{ist}^2.
\]
The two available diamonds are
\[
 D_{ac}+D_{bc}
 =\sum_{i,s,t\geq1}\theta_i^u\theta_s^{r+\ell}
       (1+\theta_s^h)\theta_t^{k+u}M_{ist}^2.
\]
For $x,y\in[0,1]$, $k\geq u$ implies
\[
 x^ky^{2u}+y^kx^{2u}
 \leq x^uy^{k+u}+y^ux^{k+u},
\]
because their difference, right minus left, is
$x^uy^u(x^u-y^u)(x^{k-u}-y^{k-u})\geq0$.
This includes $k=u$ and zero eigenvalues without division.
Symmetrize the sums in $i,t$, using $M_{ist}=M_{tsi}$.
The definition of $\gamma_{d,h}$ then gives
\[
 E_0\leq\gamma_{d,h}(D_{ac}+D_{bc}).
\]
Under the displayed density assumption, $E\leq D_{ac}+D_{bc}$.
Substitute in \eqref{eq:rank3-complete-expansion} and retain every other
nonnegative term.  This proves $F\geq1$.
For general bounded $W$, rescale by its positive supremum.
\end{proof}

The threshold is explicit.  For $d=0$, $\gamma_{0,h}=1/4$.
For $d>0$, put $b=d/h$ and
\[
 y_* =\frac{2b}{3+\sqrt{9+4b(b+1)}}.
\]
Then
\[
 \gamma_{d,h}=\frac14\,
       \frac{y_*^b(1-y_*)^2}{1+y_*}.
\]
Indeed, differentiating $y^b(1-y)^2/(1+y)$ gives the unique interior
stationary point satisfying $b-3y-(b+1)y^2=0$; the endpoint values vanish.
A simpler, weaker threshold is
\[
 c_{d,h}=\frac14\max_{0\leq x\leq1}x^d(1-x^h)^2
 =\begin{cases}
 1/4,&d=0,\\
 \displaystyle\frac{h^2}{(d+2h)^2}
       \left(\frac{d}{d+2h}\right)^{d/h},&d>0.
 \end{cases}
\]
It specializes, for $h=1$, to $c_{d,1}=d^d/(d+2)^{d+2}$.
For example, lengths $(2,6,2,4,2,2)$ are covered at $p\geq1/27$;
the two-diamond threshold is smaller.  These remain \emph{restricted}
weighted-host inequalities.  At any fixed tuple, sufficiently sparse hosts
remain untreated by this density threshold, so the proposition does not establish
\eqref{eq:rank3-entropy-target} through a universal type-host argument.

\subsection{A host-dependent mixing criterion}

The compensation proof uses $1/p$ only as an upper bound for $K_\ell$.
Keeping its actual smoothing norm gives a larger sufficient region.

\begin{proposition}[Mixing compensation]
For the same ordered tuple, write $C_\ell=\max_{x,y}K_\ell(x,y)$.
Then $F\geq1$ whenever $\gamma_{r-\ell,h}C_\ell\leq1$.
Let $\alpha=\min_x\mu_x$ and let $\beta$ be the largest centered eigenvalue
of $A$, with $\beta=0$ on a one-state space.  In particular, it suffices that
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_{r-\ell,h}\bigl[1+(\alpha^{-1}-1)\beta^\ell\bigr]\leq1.
 \label{eq:mixing-compensation}
\end{equation}
If $A$ is irreducible, this holds for all sufficiently large $\ell$,
uniformly over $k\geq u\geq1$, $r\geq\ell$ and $h\geq1$.
The required length depends on the host.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The nonnegative integrand defining $E$ gives $E\leq C_\ell E_0$.
The coefficient comparison $E_0\leq\gamma_{r-\ell,h}(D_{ac}+D_{bc})$
then proves the first assertion by the same complete centered expansion.
For a complete centered orthonormal eigenbasis, weighted completeness gives
\[
 \sum_{i\geq1}\phi_i(x)^2=\mu_x^{-1}-1.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and $0\leq\theta_i\leq\beta$ imply
\[
 |B_\ell(x,y)|\leq\beta^\ell
 \sqrt{(\mu_x^{-1}-1)(\mu_y^{-1}-1)}
 \leq(\alpha^{-1}-1)\beta^\ell.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:mixing-compensation}, without changing the
inner-product measure.  For finite irreducible $A$, the eigenvalue one
is simple and $\beta<1$.  Since $\gamma_{d,h}\leq1/4$, it is enough that
$(\alpha^{-1}-1)\beta^\ell\leq3$, which holds eventually.
\end{proof}

For reducible $A$, apply the criterion on each communicating component
with its conditional probability measure and normalized kernel.  Combining
the component bounds with the mass factor $\mu(C)^{-2}$, as below, gives
the same eventual conclusion for every fixed finite host.  Irreducibility
of $T$ alone is not sufficient: a periodic $T$ can have reducible $T^2$.
This is a host-dependent long-path statement, not the uniform all-host
inequality at fixed lengths needed for the unrestricted entropy target.

\subsection{A density-free bound for flat-spectrum hosts}

The density assumption in the compensation bound can be removed for a
restricted spectral class.  The following argument permits six arbitrary
positive exponents and does not require a nonnegative spectral projection.

\begin{proposition}[Flat-spectrum comparison]
Suppose $A=\Pi+\eta P$, where $0\leq\eta\leq1$, $P$ is an orthogonal
projection annihilating constants, and $A$ is a nonnegative self-adjoint
Markov kernel on the finite probability space.  Write $d=\operatorname{rank}P$.
For six positive integers $a_e$, put $S=\sum_ea_e$ and $m_0=\min_ea_e$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 F(a)\geq1+2d\eta^{S-3m_0}+d^2\eta^S\geq1.
 \label{eq:flat-spectrum-rank3-bound}
\end{equation}
Zero eigenspaces and repeated positive eigenvalues are allowed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $\eta>0$, $B_j=\eta^jP$.  Choose an orthonormal basis
$\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_d$ of the range of $P$ and set
\[
 \Delta=\sum_{i,s,t=1}^d\left(\int\phi_i\phi_s\phi_t\,d\mu\right)^2\geq0,
 \qquad q=\int_{\Omega^4}\prod_{vw\in E(K_4)}P(x_v,x_w)\,d\mu^4.
\]
The complete centered expansion is
\[
 F(a)-1=d\sum_{H\text{ triangle}}\eta^{\sum_{e\in H}a_e}
       +d\sum_{H\text{ four-cycle}}\eta^{\sum_{e\in H}a_e}
       +\Delta\sum_e\eta^{S-a_e}+q\eta^S.
\]
For every proper surviving core $H$, its omitted exponents are at least
$m_0$, so
$\sum_{e\in H}a_e\leq S-(6-|E(H)|)m_0$.
Its coefficient is nonnegative and $\eta\leq1$.  The full tetrahedron
term, whose sign is not assumed, has exactly the same factor on both sides
of the resulting comparison:
\[
 F(a)-1\geq\eta^{S-6m_0}
       \bigl(F(m_0,m_0,m_0,m_0,m_0,m_0)-1\bigr).
\]
For $L=A^{m_0}$ and $f_{b,d}(x)=L(x,b)L(x,d)$, the homogeneous density is
\[
 \int L(b,d)\langle f_{b,d},Lf_{b,d}\rangle\,d\mu(b)d\mu(d)
 \geq\int L(b,d)[L^2(b,d)]^2\,d\mu^2
 \geq[\operatorname{Tr}(L^3)]^2.
\]
The first inequality uses $L-\Pi\succeq0$ and $L(b,d)\geq0$ in the
original inner product.  The second is Cauchy--Schwarz under the probability
measure $L(b,d)d\mu^2$.  Since
$\operatorname{Tr}(L^3)=1+d\eta^{3m_0}$, substitution proves
\eqref{eq:flat-spectrum-rank3-bound}.  If $\eta=0$, every positive power
is $\Pi$ and $F=1$.  The final exponents $S-3m_0$ and $S$ are strictly
positive, so this case involves no $0^0$ ambiguity.  The same proof applies
at $\eta=1$.
\end{proof}

There is also a direct qualitative proof.  For $\eta>0$,
$K_j=(1-\eta^{j-1})\Pi+\eta^{j-1}A$.  Expanding the six factors makes
$F$ a convex combination of densities $t(H,A)$ for $H\subseteq K_4$.
Of the $64$ edge subsets, $38$ are forests, $16$ have triangle core,
$3$ are four-cycles, $6$ are diamonds, and one is $K_4$.
Leaf removal gives density one for forests; cycles have density
$\operatorname{Tr}(A^j)\geq1$.  A diamond has density
$\int A(b,d)[A^2(b,d)]^2d\mu^2\geq[\operatorname{Tr}(A^3)]^2$.
The preceding Gram argument bounds the $K_4$ density below by the same
quantity.  Thus every member of the convex combination is at least one.
This proof uses nonnegativity of the actual kernel $A$, not of $P$.

The bound extends componentwise.  Let $C$ run over the communicating
components of $A$, with $\alpha_C=\mu(C)>0$.  The conditional kernel is
$A_C=\alpha_CA|_{C^2}$ relative to $\mu_C=\mu|_C/\alpha_C$.
If each $A_C$ has one distinct positive nonprincipal eigenvalue
$\eta_C$ of multiplicity $d_C$ (or no such eigenvalue), connectedness of
$K_4$ gives
\[
 F(a)=\sum_C\alpha_C^{-2}F_C(a)
 \geq\sum_C\alpha_C^{-2}
       \bigl(1+2d_C\eta_C^{S-3m_0}+d_C^2\eta_C^S\bigr)\geq1.
\]
The exponent $-2$ comes from four probability factors and six kernel
factors.  No nonnegative square root of a component restriction is assumed.

\paragraph{Independent-coordinate mixtures.}
A related class permits distinct positive eigenvalues in a single
irreducible host.  On a product of finite probability spaces, take
nonnegative Markov kernels $S_j=\Pi_j+s_jP_j$, with centered orthogonal
projections $P_j$ and $0\leq s_j\leq1$.  Lift $S_j$ by averaging all
other coordinates, and choose $t_j\geq0$, $\sum_jt_j\leq1$.
Then
\[
 T=(1-\sum_jt_j)\Pi+\sum_jt_jS_j^{\rm lift},\qquad
 A=T^2=\Pi+\sum_j\theta_jP_j^{\rm lift},\quad\theta_j=(t_js_j)^2.
\]
Mixed-coordinate cubic moments vanish by independence.  Every nonempty
surviving centered core is connected, so its modes lie in one coordinate
band.  Consequently $F(a)=1+\sum_j(F_j(a)-1)$, and
\[
 F(a)\geq1+\sum_j
       \bigl(2d_j\theta_j^{S-3m_0}+d_j^2\theta_j^S\bigr),
 \qquad d_j=\operatorname{rank}P_j.
\]
The local kernel has the nonnegative root $(1-t_j)\Pi_j+t_jS_j$.
The independence hypothesis is essential to this argument: general
common-power hosts need not have vanishing mixed cubic moments.
These restricted-host proofs have analytical review only; neither Lean
validation nor novelty is claimed.

\subsection{Weighted three-state hosts and a one-spike spectrum}

The next argument permits unequal probability masses and all six positive
exponents.  Unlike the preceding coordinate construction, it need not
annihilate mixed cubic moments.  Write $I(x,y)=\mathbf1\{x=y\}/\mu_x$
for the identity kernel, and $\Pi(x,y)=1$.

\begin{proposition}[One-spike common powers]
Let $f$ be a real function with $\int f\,d\mu=0$ and
$\int f^2d\mu=1$.  Suppose
\[
 A=yI+(1-y)\Pi+(x-y)f\otimes f,\qquad 0\leq y\leq x\leq1,
\]
is entrywise nonnegative.  For arbitrary positive integers $a_e$, put
$S=\sum_ea_e$ and $M_\mu=\sum_z\mu_z^{-2}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 F(a)\geq1+y^S(M_\mu-1)
 +\sum_{\Delta\text{ triangle}}
    \prod_{e\in\Delta}(x^{a_e}-y^{a_e})
    \prod_{e\notin\Delta}(1-y^{a_e})\geq1.
 \label{eq:one-spike-common-powers}
\end{equation}
Every nonnegative self-adjoint PSD Markov kernel on a positive weighted
three-state space has this form.  On a space with at least two states,
equality $F(a)=1$ holds exactly when $A=\Pi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $y=1$, then $x=1$ and $A=I$.  The density is $M_\mu$ and the
displayed bound is exact.  Suppose $y<1$.  For each $m\geq1$,
\[
 A^m=y^mI+(1-y^m)S_m,\qquad
 S_m=\Pi+\tau_m f\otimes f,\qquad
 \tau_m=\frac{x^m-y^m}{1-y^m}.
\]
Here $0\leq\tau_m\leq\tau_1$: after factoring $x-y$ and $1-y$,
the geometric sums compare termwise because
$x^{m-1-j}y^j\leq y^j$.  This also includes $x=y$, when every
$\tau_m$ is zero.  Off the diagonal, $S_1=A/(1-y)\geq0$;
on the diagonal $S_1(z,z)=1+\tau_1f(z)^2\geq1$.
Each $S_m$ is therefore a nonnegative convex combination of $\Pi$ and
$S_1$, with diagonal at least one.

Expand the six edge kernels into their $I/S_{a_e}$ choices.  Their
coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one.  We show that every such
colored density is at least one.  In the graph of identity edges, retain
a spanning forest of each component.  On the support of that forest,
each deleted identity-cycle factor is $1/\mu_z\geq1$; outside its
support the retained product is zero.  All other factors are nonnegative,
so deletion gives a lower bound.  Contract the forest.  A component of
$v$ vertices has $v-1$ retained identity edges, leaving exactly one
factor $\mu_z^v\mu_z^{-(v-1)}=\mu_z$ in its sum.  Thus contraction
preserves the original probability measure at each quotient vertex.
Discard any resulting $S_m$ loop, whose diagonal factor is at least one.

The remaining loopless multigraph comes from a partition of the four
vertices of $K_4$.  A quotient vertex containing $s$ original vertices
has degree at most $s(4-s)\leq4$.  Expand each remaining edge factor as
$1+\tau_m f(z)f(w)$.  A chosen edge set contributes a nonnegative
coefficient times a product of moments
$m_j=\int f^j d\mu$, with $0\leq j\leq4$.
Terms with degree one vanish because $m_1=0$.
The moments $m_0,m_2,m_4$ are nonnegative, and the only remaining odd
degree is three.  The handshake identity forces an even number of
degree-three vertices, so the common moment $m_3$ occurs to an even
power.  Every surviving term is nonnegative.  The empty edge set gives
one, proving the qualitative bound for each choice.

The all-identity choice has coefficient $y^S$ and density $M_\mu$.
In the all-$S$ choice, each triangle contributes
$\prod_{e\in\Delta}\tau_{a_e}$, since $m_2=1$.
Retaining these disjoint surplus contributions gives
\eqref{eq:one-spike-common-powers}.
On three states the centered subspace has dimension two; choose its
orthonormal eigenfunctions with eigenvalues $x\geq y$.
Its spectral decomposition is precisely the stated form.
For at least two states, $M_\mu>1$.  If $y>0$ the all-identity surplus
is positive; if $y=0<x$, every triangle surplus is positive.
Only $x=y=0$, equivalently $A=\Pi$, permits equality.
The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

For the original host $A=T^2$, self-adjointness shows that $A=\Pi$
is equivalent to $T=\Pi$, hence to the constant kernel $W=p$.
Negative centered eigenvalues of $T$ are allowed: only their squares
enter the argument.  A one-state host gives $F=1$ directly, without a
centered normalized function.  In higher dimension the proposition
allows at most one distinguished one-dimensional direction above a common
eigenvalue on the remaining centered subspace, including the flat case $x=y$.
It does not cover arbitrary
interacting spectra or assert positivity of individual spectral prefixes.

For an exact sparse example, take
\[
 \mu=(1/2,16/33,1/66),\qquad f=(3/5,-2/5,-7),\qquad
 T=\tfrac34I+\tfrac14\Pi+\tfrac1{20}f\otimes f.
\]
This is a strictly positive Markov kernel with centered eigenvalues
$4/5,3/4$ and maximum $261/5$.  Thus $W=(5/261)T$ is an admissible
regular host.  At $a=(4,3,1,2,1,1)$, the previous density condition fails:
$\gamma_{1,1}\geq9/320>5/261$.  Nevertheless
\[
 F(a)=\frac{31870316227507292660361657490451130365121}
             {1677721600000000000000000000000000000000}>1.
\]
The tuple avoids the three equal-spoke slices listed earlier.  This
evaluation is a reproducible diagnostic, not the proof; the proposition
establishes the whole three-state class.  Common exponents are already
covered without a state-count restriction by Sidorenko's complete-graph
inequality for doubly nonnegative kernels, Corollary~5.2 of the paper cited
above.  The additional statement here concerns six unequal positive integer
powers.  A bounded literature check does not establish its novelty.

\subsection{Nested conditional-expectation hosts}

The preceding coordinate argument eliminates mixed cubic moments.
A partition hierarchy gives a different sufficient structure that allows
those moments to be nonzero.  Its proof works for every finite simple graph,
not only the $K_4$ core.

\begin{proposition}[Hierarchical common powers]
Let $\mathcal P_0=\{\Omega\},\ldots,\mathcal P_L$ be nested partitions
of the finite positive probability space, ordered by refinement.  Set
\[
 E_j(x,y)=\frac{\mathbf1\{x,y\text{ lie in the same }\mathcal P_j
                         \text{ cell}\}}{\mu(\mathcal P_j(x))},
 \qquad T=\sum_{j=0}^Lw_jE_j,
 \quad w_j\geq0,\quad\sum_jw_j=1.
\]
For every finite simple graph $H$ and positive integer exponents $a_e$,
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\Omega^{V(H)}}\prod_{e=vw\in E(H)}
              T^{2a_e}(x_v,x_w)\,d\mu^{V(H)}\geq1.
 \label{eq:hierarchical-common-powers}
\end{equation}
Unequal cell masses, zero weights and disconnected hosts are allowed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First assign to each edge a level $c_e\in\{0,\ldots,L\}$ and prove the
bound for the kernels $E_{c_e}$ by induction on the hierarchy depth.
At depth zero every kernel equals one.  At positive depth, remove the
level-zero edges and factor the integral over the connected components
of the remaining graph.  Isolated vertices contribute one.
For a component $Q$ with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges, nonzero products
place all its vertices in one cell $C$ of $\mathcal P_1$.  Write
$\alpha_C=\mu(C)$ and $\mu_C=\mu|_C/\alpha_C$.
Each of its $m$ kernels equals $\alpha_C^{-1}$ times the corresponding
local kernel at level $c_e-1$, while its $n$ measure factors contribute
$\alpha_C^n$.  Its exact density is therefore
\[
 \sum_{C\in\mathcal P_1}\alpha_C^{n-m}
       t\bigl(Q;(E_{c_e-1}^{C})\bigr).
\]
The induction hypothesis bounds every local density below by one.
Connectedness gives $m\geq n-1$, so
$\alpha_C^{n-m}\geq\alpha_C$ and the displayed sum is at least one.
Multiplication over components proves the colored-kernel assertion.

The conditional-expectation tower identity is
$E_iE_j=E_{\min(i,j)}$.  Thus every positive power of $T$ is a convex
combination of the same kernels.  More explicitly, if
$q_j=\sum_{i\geq j}w_i$ and $q_{L+1}=0$, its coefficient at $E_j$ in
$T^s$ is $q_j^s-q_{j+1}^s$.  These coefficients are nonnegative and sum
to one.  Expanding the edge powers makes the left side of
\eqref{eq:hierarchical-common-powers} a convex combination of the
colored densities just bounded below by one.
\end{proof}

For example, take masses $(1/2,2/5,1/10)$, the partitions
$\{\Omega\}$, $\{\{1\},\{2,3\}\}$ and the discrete partition, and
$T=E_0/10+E_1/10+4E_2/5$.  The kernel has maximum $83/10$, so
$W=(10/83)T$ is an admissible regular host.  The two centered eigenvalues
of $A$ are $81/100$ and $16/25$.  For normalized coarse and fine
eigenfunctions $\phi=(-1,1,1)$ and
$\psi=(0,1/\sqrt2,-2\sqrt2)$, the mixed moment
$\int\phi\psi^2d\mu=1$ is nonzero.
At $(3,2,1,1,1,1)$, both the original density threshold and the
mixing-norm criterion fail, but the hierarchical theorem applies.
An arbitrary common-power host need not admit this hierarchy;
commuting projections alone do not provide it.  This is an analytically
reviewed control class, not a claim of novelty or an unrestricted entropy
construction.

\subsection{An exact binary entropy construction}

Let $\kappa$ be uniform on $\{-1,1\}$ and
$\lambda_\rho(s,t)=(1+\rho st)/4$.  Write
\[
 I(\rho)=\frac{1+\rho}{2}\log(1+\rho)
          +\frac{1-\rho}{2}\log(1-\rho).
\]
The following criterion concerns prescribed marginals, not merely a
homomorphism count with one common edge potential.

\begin{proposition}[A binary path criterion]
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph and $0\leq r<1$.
For each edge $e=vw$, let $\mathcal P_e$ be the simple $v$--$w$ paths in $G-e$.
If $\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_e}r^{|\pi|}\leq r$ for every $e$, there exists
a law $\tau$ on $\{-1,1\}^{V(G)}$ with every edge marginal $\lambda_r$ and
$D(\tau\Vert\kappa^{V(G)})\leq |E(G)|I(r)$.
For bipartite $G$, the same holds with $\lambda_{-r}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $r=0$, use independent spins.  Otherwise put $b=\operatorname{atanh}r$
and minimize the continuous function
\[
 \Phi(\beta)=\log Z(\beta)-r\sum_e\beta_e,\qquad
 Z(\beta)=2^{-|V(G)|}\sum_\sigma
      \exp\left(\sum_{e=vw}\beta_e\sigma_v\sigma_w\right)
\]
on the compact box $[0,b]^{E(G)}$.
With $t_e=\tanh\beta_e$, the finite high-temperature expansion is
\[
 Z(\beta)=\prod_e\cosh\beta_e
       \sum_{A\subseteq E(G):\,\partial A=\varnothing}\prod_{e\in A}t_e
       \geq\prod_e\cosh\beta_e.
\]
In $G-e$, its endpoint correlation $c_e$ lies between zero and
$\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_e}\prod_{f\in\pi}t_f\leq r$.
To justify the path bound, every set with odd boundary $\{v,w\}$ contains
a simple $v$--$w$ path.  Remove one such path; the remaining set is even.
Summing over all paths and even remainders overcounts the nonnegative
odd-boundary numerator and gives the asserted bound after division by
the even-subgraph denominator.
Reinserting $e$ gives correlation $(t_e+c_e)/(1+t_ec_e)$ and
$\partial_e\Phi=\mathbb E(\sigma_v\sigma_w)-r$.
At a lower box face this derivative is nonpositive, whereas a constrained
minimum requires it to be nonnegative.  At an upper face it is
nonnegative, whereas a minimum requires it to be nonpositive.
All derivatives thus vanish at a minimizer $\beta^*$.
The resulting Gibbs law has correlation $r$ on each edge.
Global spin-flip symmetry fixes uniform singletons, hence the exact law
$\lambda_r$ on every edge.  Finally,
\[
 D(\tau\Vert\kappa^{V(G)})
 =r\sum_e\beta_e^*-\log Z(\beta^*)
 \leq\sum_e\bigl(r\beta_e^*-\log\cosh\beta_e^*\bigr)
 \leq |E(G)|I(r).
\]
The last bound is the elementary Legendre transform of $\log\cosh$.
Flip the spins on one bipartition class to obtain $-r$.
\end{proof}

In a $K_4$ subdivision, deletion of an actual edge leaves exactly four
alternate simple paths between its endpoints: after the forced path tails,
they are the core routes $acb,adb,acdb,adcb$ for the relevant branch edge $ab$.
For a simple bipartite graph each has length at least three.
Thus $4r^3\leq r$ proves the criterion for $r\leq1/2$.
The uniform binary pair law has an exchangeable triple extension exactly
when $-1/3\leq\rho\leq1$: necessity follows from
$(\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3)^2\geq1$, and sufficiency from
\[
 \eta_\rho(x,y,z)=\frac18\bigl(1+\rho(xy+xz+yz)\bigr).
\]
Consequently \eqref{eq:rank3-entropy-target} holds for this specified
binary family when $-1/3\leq\rho\leq1/2$.
This source-reviewed result is not being claimed as novel; exact
literature reconciliation is a separate obligation.
The argument belongs to established Ising and Bethe methodology.
Ruozzi proves a partition-function lower bound for binary
log-supermodular models,\footnote{N.~Ruozzi,
\emph{The Bethe Partition Function of Log-supermodular Graphical Models},
Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 25 (2012),
\url{https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/03afdbd66e7929b125f8597834fa83a4-Paper.pdf}.}
whereas attractive Ising fitting permits excess fitted correlation on
zero-coupling edges.\footnote{S.~Lauritzen, C.~Uhler and P.~Zwiernik,
\emph{Total positivity in exponential families with application to binary variables},
Corollary 3.7 and Remark 3.9,
\url{https://web.math.ku.dk/~lauritzen/papers/AOS2007.pdf}.}
Neither statement alone supplies all the exact prescribed marginals here;
the path criterion discharges that additional obligation.

\subsection{Two limits on proposed constructions}

\paragraph{Direct mixture inheritance fails.}
Mix, with weights $1/2,1/2$, the iid uniform binary triple and the triple
that is $000$ or $111$ with equal probabilities.  The result is strictly
positive, with masses $5/16$ at $000,111$ and $1/16$ elsewhere, and pair
law $\lambda_{1/2}$.
For the iid component a budget-satisfying extension is forced to be iid,
since its allowed divergence is zero.  For the diagonal component,
connectedness forces the unique law supported on $0^n,1^n$.
Their direct mixture is therefore
\[
 \tau_{\rm mix}=\tfrac12\kappa^n
       +\tfrac14\delta_{0^n}+\tfrac14\delta_{1^n},\qquad
 D(\tau_{\rm mix}\Vert\kappa^n)
       =(\tfrac12+2^{-n})\log(2^{n-1}+1)-\log2.
\]
For $J_0=J(2,4,3,2,3,1)$, $n=13,m=15$, and
\[
 D(\tau_{\rm mix}\Vert\kappa^{13})>5\log2
       >15I(1/2)=15(\tfrac34\log3-\log2).
\]
The first comparison uses $4097>2^{12}$ and $4097/8192>1/2$;
the second is equivalent to $2^{80}>3^{45}$, which follows already
from $2^{16}>3^9$.
The binary construction above supplies a \emph{different} successful
extension for this same law.  Thus this is a counterexample to direct
mixture inheritance, not to the target.

For components sharing $\kappa$, let
$\delta_z=mD(\lambda_z\Vert\kappa^2)-D(\tau_z\Vert\kappa^n)$.
The exact chain rule is
\[
 D(\tau_{\rm mix}\Vert\kappa^n)-mD(\lambda_{\rm mix}\Vert\kappa^2)
 =mI(Z;X_v,X_w)-I(Z;X_V)-\sum_z\alpha_z\delta_z.
\]
The example violates the desired sign even after component slack is included.
It does not decide convexity of the class of pair laws admitting some
successful extension.

\paragraph{Commuting positive kernels are not common powers.}
Index six signs $r_e\in\{-1,1\}$ by the edges of $K_4$, and put
$S_v(r)=\prod_{e\ni v}r_e$.
On these sign vectors define
\[
 \mu(r)=2^{-6}\left(1+\frac{S_a(r)+S_b(r)+S_c(r)-S_d(r)}2\right).
\]
Since $S_aS_bS_cS_d=1$, the signed sum is $2$ or $-2$.
After removing null states this is uniform on $32$ states.
The six coordinates are centered and orthonormal: averaging their
degree-one and degree-two characters against this density gives zero.
Each $K_e(x,y)=1+r_e(x)r_e(y)$ is a symmetric nonnegative Markov
projection; the six projections commute.
In the coloured $K_4$ density every proper nonempty centered term
vanishes at a vertex of degree one or two.  The full term is
\[
 (\mathbb E S_a)(\mathbb E S_b)(\mathbb E S_c)(\mathbb E S_d)
 =(\tfrac12)^3(-\tfrac12)=-\tfrac1{16}.
\]
The density is $15/16<1$.
Positive powers of one self-adjoint operator have a common nullspace,
whereas these projections have distinct nullspaces.
Each individual pair law even has an exchangeable triple extension:
sample its common coordinate sign and then three iid states conditional
on that sign.  What is absent is the \emph{same} pair law and common-power
relationship.  This exact example forbids that relaxation, not the target.

\subsection{The unresolved common-power comparison}

For $a=(k,r+h,u,r,u,\ell)$, the sufficient inequality
\[
 \int K_\ell(z,w)\langle
 B_{r+h}(\cdot,z)B_u(\cdot,w),
 B_k[B_r(\cdot,z)B_u(\cdot,w)]\rangle\,d\mu^2\geq0
\]
would settle this particular all-even family on every normalized weighted
host: its left side is $Q+D_{cd}$.
It is unproved here and stronger than what the compensation proposition
needs.  The factor $K_\ell(z,w)$ changes the inner-product measure;
positivity and self-adjointness in the original measure do not justify
a change of measure.  The following example shows that this is an actual
obstruction to the arbitrary-vector argument, even for common powers.

\paragraph{Weighted positivity fails on an admissible common-power host.}
Take three states with uniform measure, $p=6/13$, and
\[
 W=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&1/13&4/13\\
 1/13&1&4/13\\
 4/13&4/13&10/13
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 P=\begin{pmatrix}
 13/18&1/18&2/9\\
 1/18&13/18&2/9\\
 2/9&2/9&5/9
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here $W$ is symmetric, strictly positive, bounded by one, and has row
mean $p$.  The matrix $P$ represents the operator $T=W/p$;
the kernel is $3P$, because composition uses the uniform probability
measure.  Thus $A$ is represented by $P^2$, with eigenvalues
$1,4/9,1/9$.  Set $v=(-1,-3,4)$ and choose the first state as $w$.
Since $v$ is mean zero and both nonconstant eigenvalues are positive,
$v$ belongs to the range of $B_1$.
Direct rational arithmetic gives
\[
 A^3v=\frac1{729}(62,-66,4),\qquad
 K_1(\cdot,w)=\left(\frac{31}{18},\frac7{18},\frac89\right),
\]
and hence
\[
 \int K_1(z,w)v(z)(A^3v)(z)\,d\mu(z)
       =-\frac{140}{19683}<0.
\]
Common powers and smoothing therefore do not imply weighted positivity
for arbitrary mean-zero inputs.  This does not refute $Q+D_{cd}\geq0$:
the inputs in that expression are products of eigenfunctions and kernel
sections, summed with prescribed spectral weights.  Nor does it refute
$F\geq1$, which retains the other nonnegative terms in
\eqref{eq:rank3-complete-expansion}.

\paragraph{A comparison retaining all proper terms.}
For each nonconstant eigenfunction define
\[
 R_s=\int\phi_s(a)\phi_s(c)
 K_k(a,b)K_u(a,d)K_r(b,c)K_u(b,d)K_\ell(c,d)\,d\mu^4.
\]
Expanding only the $ac$ kernel gives the exact identity
\[
 F=R_0+\sum_{s\geq1}\theta_s^{r+h}R_s,
\]
where $R_0$ denotes the same integral with $\phi_0=1$.
The remaining five-edge graph is an uncentered diamond, so
\[
 R_0=1+\sum_{i\geq1}\theta_i^{k+2u}
       +\sum_{i\geq1}\theta_i^{r+u+\ell}
       +\sum_{i\geq1}\theta_i^{k+r+u+\ell}+D_{ac}\geq1.
\]
Group the distinct positive nonconstant eigenvalues as
$\eta_1>\cdots>\eta_q>0$, and put
$c_j=\eta_j^r\sum_{\theta_s=\eta_j}R_s$ and
$S_j=\sum_{v=1}^jc_v$.  These grouped sums do not depend on the choice
of basis inside a repeated eigenspace.  For $q\geq1$, Abel summation gives
\[
 F-R_0=\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}(\eta_j^h-\eta_{j+1}^h)S_j
             +\eta_q^hS_q.
\]
If $q=0$, every positive kernel power is $\Pi$ and $F=R_0=1$.
The auxiliary value $F_0$ obtained by setting $h=0$ has matched spokes
at $a,b$.  The same Gram argument gives
$F_0\geq[\operatorname{Tr}(A^{\ell+r+u})]^2\geq1$.
Since $F_0=R_0+S_q$, Abel summation can instead be written as
\[
 F_h=(1-\eta_1^h)R_0
     +\sum_{j<q}(\eta_j^h-\eta_{j+1}^h)(R_0+S_j)
     +\eta_q^hF_0.
\]
The weights are nonnegative and sum to one.  It would therefore suffice
to prove $R_0+S_j\geq1$ for every proper positive spectral prefix $j<q$.
This is weaker than $S_j\geq0$ and retains the proper-term slack.
Neither condition is proved for general hosts.  An exact four-state
host found during the diagnostic pass has an individual $R_s<0$, but
all its old and shifted prefixes are positive; it refutes individual
coefficient positivity only, not this remaining route or the target.
The necessary comparison is
$\sum_{s\geq1}\theta_s^{r+h}R_s\geq-(R_0-1)$, with the actual common
host and moment constraints retained.

\paragraph{An exact dissipation identity, not a completed comparison.}
Include the constant mode $\phi_0=1$, $\theta_0=1$, and define
\[
 g_i(c,d)=\int\phi_i(x)K_r(x,c)K_u(x,d)d\mu(x),\qquad
 H(s)=\sum_i\theta_i^k\int K_s(c,d)g_i(c,d)^2d\mu^2.
\]
Let $P_h$ act in the $c$ coordinate by the actual kernel $K_h$ and put
\[
 D_h=\sum_i\theta_i^k\int K_\ell(c,d)K_h(c,c')
          [g_i(c,d)-g_i(c',d)]^2d\mu(c)d\mu(c')d\mu(d).
\]
Complete spectral expansion of $K_k$ and polarization give
\begin{equation}
 F_h=\tfrac12[H(\ell)+H(\ell+h)-D_h],\qquad D_h\geq0.
 \label{eq:actual-source-dissipation}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the two square terms integrate to $H(\ell)$ and $H(\ell+h)$
by the Markov and composition identities, and the cross term is $2F_h$.
The matched-spoke Gram argument gives
$H(s)\geq[\operatorname{Tr}(A^{s+r+u})]^2\geq1$.
Nevertheless the needed upper bound
$D_h\leq H(\ell)+H(\ell+h)-2$ is exactly equivalent to $F_h\geq1$;
the identity does not prove that budget.  Markov covariance positivity
instead supplies a lower bound on part of $D_h$, in the wrong direction.

Even positive functions with both weighted marginals equal to one do not
suffice for a generic replacement of the source functions.  The fixed
four-state example in \texttt{pass4\_checks.py}, with masses
$(1/6,1/6,1/3,1/3)$, uses an admissible common-power host and
$g(c,d)=1+f(c)q(d)/8$, where
$f=(-1,-1,-3,4)$ and $q=(1,-1,0,0)$.
Although $1/2\leq g\leq3/2$, both marginals equal one, and both
reflection square integrals exceed one, its cross integral at
$\ell=1,h=3$ is $1-35/944784<1$.
This is not a counterexample to the density target: it is not the actual
source family $g_i=A^r\operatorname{diag}(\phi_i)A^u$.
A valid proof must retain that shared source structure and its actual
moment constraints.

For arbitrary interacting spectra, six independent exponents and odd-parity
paths require still further control.  The unrestricted entropy theorem,
formalization of these analytical results, global novelty and B-level
significance remain open obligations rather than conclusions of this note.
