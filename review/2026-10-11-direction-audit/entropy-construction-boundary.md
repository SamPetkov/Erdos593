# Exact edge entropy and a failed bridge construction

The length-two subdivision of \(K_4\) has an exact prescribed-edge entropy
witness for every finite pair table. This is known coverage, not a newly
solved graph class. A different, natural construction fits the same tables
but can overspend the entropy budget, even on a positive three-symbol input.
The distinction matters when seeking a mechanism for unequal path lengths.

## Known coverage and a finite dual proof

Let \(G\) be the incidence graph between four points and six unordered pairs.
It has twelve edges, with left degree three and right degree two. Orient every
edge left-to-right. For any positive law \(\kappa\) on a finite nonempty
alphabet and any ordered pair law \(\lambda\) with both marginals \(\kappa\),
there is a joint law \(\tau\) whose individual edge tables are all
\(\lambda\), whose singleton laws are all \(\kappa\), and which satisfies

\[
D(\tau\Vert\kappa^{10})\le12D(\lambda\Vert\kappa^2).
\tag{1}
\]

Symmetry and an exchangeable triple extension are not needed for this graph.
The scalar primitive is

\[
t_G(W)\ge(\mathbb E_{\kappa^2}W)^{12}
\tag{2}
\]

for bounded nonnegative, possibly nonsymmetric \(W\). This is the tetrahedron
point-edge incidence instance of Conlon–Lee's Theorem 1.1, or their
reflection-graph Theorem 1.2 with \(S_4\) parabolics generated by
\(\{s_1,s_2\}\) and \(\{s_1,s_3\}\); their equation (6) gives the scalar
inequality. A step function on intervals of lengths \(\kappa_x\) transports
the stated Lebesgue-law theorem to the original finite law exactly.
See [the authors' paper](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~dconlon/norming.pdf).

More generally, universally quantified scalar Sidorenko implies the exact
individual-edge entropy property by Szegedy's
[Corollary 9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1504.00858).
This correspondence was already recorded in the research packet. Neither
(1) nor the clique subdivision is offered as a new research advance.

Here is a finite alternative derivation useful for tracing the unary fields.
For positive \(\lambda\), choose
\(0<\epsilon<\min(1,\min_{x,y}\lambda_{xy}/(\kappa_x\kappa_y))\), and
set \(\lambda'=(\lambda-\epsilon\kappa^2)/(1-\epsilon)\).
Assign one sampled \(X\) to all left vertices and one sampled \(Y\) to all
right vertices, with \((X,Y)\sim\lambda'\), and mix this law with
\(\epsilon\kappa^{10}\). This is strictly positive and feasible.

The unique constrained KL minimizer has full support: mixing a boundary
minimizer with the strictly positive witness would give a negative
\(s\log s\) first-order contribution at its zero entries. Finite affine
optimality therefore represents its log density by unary and edge potentials.
Average these potentials under the side-preserving \(S_4\) automorphisms.
Uniqueness ensures that the density itself is invariant, so the result has
common left field \(\alpha\), right field \(\beta\), and oriented edge
potential \(\phi\). With

\[
W(x,y)=\exp[\phi(x,y)+\alpha(x)/3+\beta(y)/2],
\]

the normalization is exactly \(t_G(W)\), and the prescribed marginals give

\[
D(\tau\Vert\kappa^{10})
=12\mathbb E_\lambda\log W-\log t_G(W)
\le12[\mathbb E_\lambda\log W-\log\mathbb E_{\kappa^2}W]
\le12D(\lambda\Vert\kappa^2).
\]

The last step is the finite Gibbs variational inequality. For zeros in
\(\lambda\), approximate by \((1-\epsilon)\lambda+\epsilon\kappa^2\),
take a compact subsequence of feasible joint laws, and use continuity of KL
against the fixed positive reference. This preserves every edge separately,
not merely an average. The single-symbol case has zero KL.

## Why a feasible bridge law can exceed the budget

Let \(P\) be a stationary reversible transition matrix, now in ordinary
row-probability convention, and write

\[
\lambda_{xy}=\kappa_xP_{xy},\quad
I=D(\lambda\Vert\kappa^2),\quad I_2=D(\kappa P^2\Vert\kappa^2).
\]

Sample \(Z\sim\kappa\), then four conditionally IID branch colors through
\(P(Z,\cdot)\). Their pair law is \(\kappa P^2\). Independently given the
four colors, attach each of six midpoints with conditional law

\[
Q(y\mid x,x')=
\frac{\kappa_yP_{yx}P_{yx'}}{\kappa_x(P^2)_{xx'}}.
\tag{3}
\]

Define (3) arbitrarily on endpoint pairs of zero probability. Every path is
a stationary two-step \(P\) path, hence all twelve edge laws are exactly
\(\lambda\). If \(\mathrm{TC}_4\) denotes the KL of the four-branch law
against \(\kappa^4\), conditional entropy gives the exact identity

\[
D(\tau_{\mathrm{bridge}}\Vert\kappa^{10})
=12I+\mathrm{TC}_4-6I_2.
\tag{4}
\]

Indeed, each midpoint entropy conditional on its endpoints is
\(H(\kappa)-2I+I_2\), and the six midpoints are conditionally independent.
Thus feasibility does not supply the missing inequality
\(\mathrm{TC}_4\le6I_2\).

## A positive three symbol certificate

Take

\[
\kappa=(1/20,1/20,9/10),\quad g=(4,2,-1/3),\quad
f=g/\sqrt{11/10},\quad t=10^{-6},
\]

and define

\[
P_{xy}=\kappa_y(1+tf_xf_y),\qquad
\eta_{xyz}=\kappa_x\kappa_y\kappa_z
 [1+t(f_xf_y+f_xf_z+f_yf_z)].
\tag{5}
\]

Here \(\mathbb Ef=0\), \(\mathbb Ef^2=1\). All pair and triple entries
are rational and positive. The pair bracket is at least \(1-40t/33\);
the triple bracket is at least \(1-230t/99\), since the minimum sum of
the three unnormalized pair products is \(-23/9\). Equation (5) is
exchangeable and has precisely \(\lambda=\kappa P\) as every pair marginal.
The three values of \(f\) are distinct and nonzero. For every nontrivial
deterministic partition \(\{a\},\{b,c\}\),
\(P(b,\{a\})-P(c,\{a\})=\kappa_atf_a(f_b-f_c)\ne0\).
This excludes strong lumping to two blocks, not unrelated mixtures.

Set \(\mu_3=\mathbb Ef^3\), \(\mu_4=\mathbb Ef^4\). Direct moments give

\[
\mu_3^2=114490/11979,\qquad \mu_4=12250/1089<12.
\]

For four independent reference colors, set
\(A_4=\sum_{i<j}f_if_j\),
\(B_4=\sum_{i<j<k}f_if_jf_k\), and \(C_4=f_1f_2f_3f_4\).
The four-branch density is \(1+u\), where

\[
u=t^2A_4+\mu_3t^3B_4+\mu_4t^4C_4.
\]

The moments are
\(\mathbb EA_4^2=6\), \(\mathbb EB_4^2=4\),
\(\mathbb EC_4^2=1\), with all cross second moments zero, and
\(\mathbb EA_4^3=24+6\mu_3^2\).
Therefore \(\mathbb Eu^2=6t^4+4\mu_3^2t^6+\mu_4^2t^8\).
The \(24\) in the third moment is the four triangles with six edge orderings;
the other six terms are repeated edges.

For \(t\le10^{-3}\), \(|A_4|\le96\) and
\(|u-t^2A_4|\le1028t^3\). Expansion of the cube yields

\[
\frac{|\mathbb Eu^3-t^6\mathbb EA_4^3|}{6}
\le4737024t^7+50725632t^8+\frac{1086373952}{6}t^9.
\tag{6}
\]

The fourth derivative of \(\psi(x)=(1+x)\log(1+x)\) is
\(2/(1+x)^3>0\). Since \(\mathbb Eu=0\), its Taylor remainder gives
\(\mathrm{TC}_4\ge\mathbb Eu^2/2-\mathbb Eu^3/6\).
Meanwhile \((P^2)_{xy}=\kappa_y(1+t^2f_xf_y)\), so a fourth-order upper bound gives,
at \(t=10^{-6}\),

\[
6I_2\le3t^4-\mu_3^2t^6+
 \frac{\mu_4^2t^8}{2(1-16t^2)^3}
<3t^4-\mu_3^2t^6+144t^8.
\]

The total error in the difference, divided by \(t^6\), is at most

\[
4737024t+50725776t^2+\frac{1086373952}{6}t^3<5.
\]

All comparisons here are rational. Consequently

\[
\mathrm{TC}_4-6I_2>
\frac{121169}{11979}t^6>10t^6>0.
\tag{7}
\]

By (4), this feasible construction exceeds the \(12I\) budget. It is a
counterexample only to the construction: the known entropy theorem supplies
a different under-budget law on this graph. A useful unequal-path mechanism
must change the global branch/interior coupling and save actual higher-order
information; compatible individual bridges alone do not do so.
