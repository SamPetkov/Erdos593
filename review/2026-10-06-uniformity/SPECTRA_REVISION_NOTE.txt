1. EDITORIAL ASSESSMENT

The manuscript now follows one mathematical line from the finite obligatory
classification to controlled infinite avoiding hosts. The previous continuation
established the classification in every fixed finite uniformity. This revision
asks the next question: can a forbidden system be avoided while prescribing
the host's exact uncountable chromatic number and controlling its order?

The resulting Section 12 gives complete proofs of an all-cardinal dichotomy,
a bounded-intersection calibration theorem, a finite-linear-family theorem,
and a mixed-family counterexample. The introduction states the dichotomy as
Theorem C. The title is now "Obligatory uniform hypergraphs: canonical atoms
and exact spectra", covering both finite structural and chromatic spectra.

This is a stronger publication case than an independent proof of the triple
classification accompanied by finite corollaries. The case rests on the
all-uniformity trace theorem and its exact-cardinal consequences. It remains
conditional on specialist review of the proofs and priority. It is not an
acceptance forecast. The 57-page length is the main remaining editorial cost.

2. THE RETAINED HIGHER-UNIFORMITY RESULT

Theorem B and Theorem 11.1 classify finite simple r-uniform hypergraphs for
every fixed r >= 3. A finite F is obligatory precisely when it belongs to B_r,
the class generated by private r-expansions of finite bipartite graphs and
finite edgeless systems under disjoint unions and one-point amalgamations.
Equivalently, after isolated vertices are removed, F is linear, all its Berge
cycles are even, and each hyperedge-node of its Levi graph has at least r-2
incident bridges. Embeddings are injective and non-induced. Chromatic number
uses vertex colourings with no monochromatic edge.

The hypergraph-base finite trace lemma and its iteration prove the new negative
direction. Reiher supplies the already known positive generators. The canonical
atoms are single r-edges and private r-expansions of finite 2-connected bipartite
graphs. The finite structural spectra transfer using

  s = n-(r-2)m,                 beta = (r-1)m-n+c.

Section 11, including its proof, bridge-threshold example and finite transfer,
is unchanged from the preceding extension draft. Sections 1-10 retain the
original prose exactly.

3. EXACT CHROMATIC CONTROL OF THE LIFT

Lemma 12.1 proves, for every infinite cardinal kappa and finite-uniform
hypergraph H of rank at least two with nonempty vertex set,

  chi(L_kappa(H)) = min{chi(H), kappa}.

The two upper colourings use the base vertex colour and the ordinal length of
the sequence node. For the lower bound, a colouring below both cardinals
selects a monochromatic base edge at every node of a branch of length kappa.
Two stages have the same selected colour and produce a monochromatic lifted
edge. This recursion works at singular heights: the node at stage alpha has
ordinal length alpha, and alpha < kappa at every used stage.

The earlier finite linear trace proof depends only on the prefix order and
finitely many active nodes, so it applies without change at arbitrary infinite
height. At kappa = mu+, put rho = 2^mu. A hypergraph of order at most rho has
at most rho finite edges, and

  |E(H)^{<mu+}| <= mu+ (2^mu)^mu = 2^mu.

Thus the order stays at most 2^mu through any finite number of lifts. No
identity of the form (kappa^{<kappa})^{<kappa}=kappa^{<kappa} is used at
singular cardinals.

4. SIMULTANEOUS AVOIDANCE FOR FINITE LINEAR FAMILIES

Theorem 12.2 states that, for a finite family F of finite linear r-uniform
systems, the following are equivalent:

  - no member belongs to B_r;
  - at every uncountable kappa, an F-free r-uniform host exists with
    chi(H)=kappa and |V(H)| <= 2^{<kappa};
  - an uncountably chromatic F-free host exists.

The ambient hosts need not be linear. For each forbidden member, the proof
selects a connected component outside B_r. A single high-odd-girth starting
graph then excludes all these components after lifting. At a limit cardinal,
disjoint unions of cofinal successor witnesses preserve avoidance because
each selected component is connected. This reduction is essential when an
original forbidden member is disconnected. The empty family causes no exception.

The graph input has exactly the needed cardinal strength: Erdős-Hajnal,
Theorem 7.4, p.76, supplies a graph of both order and chromatic number kappa
with any prescribed finite initial segment of odd cycle lengths absent. EGH
also records the statement as Theorem C, p.428.

5. EXACT CALIBRATION WITH BOUNDED EDGE INTERSECTIONS

Theorem 12.4 proves that, for infinite mu, r >= 3 and 1 <= i <= r-2, setting

  t = ceil((r-1)/i)

gives a simple r-uniform host L with

  |e intersect f| <= i for distinct edges,
  chi(L) = mu+,
  |V(L)| <= beth_t(mu).

Here beth_0(mu)=mu and beth_{j+1}(mu)=2^{beth_j(mu)}. The linear case i=1
has order bound beth_{r-1}(mu).

Lemma 12.3 extends Li's reservoir method by adding d old vertices to each
labelled k-edge. The base uses EGH's simultaneous-colour property P: under
every colouring with fewer than mu+ colours, one colour contains base edges
of every label. A preassigned level colouring provides an explicit proper
mu+-colouring of the output. The reservoir capture proof excludes every
smaller colouring.

The technical points checked in the proof are the capacity for all reservoir
families, disjoint old vertex sets within each installed copy, all three edge
intersection cases, the common colour across all labels, and capture away
from all small colour classes. The latter uses cf(sigma)>mu; König's theorem
supplies it for the EGH input sigma=beth_{t-1}(mu), even for singular mu.

The efficient source parameters are

  k=r-i,       j=min(i,k-1),       m=t-1.

They satisfy 1 <= j < k <= mj+1, so EGH Corollary 12.3 applies directly.
Taking d=i gives rank r and adds only one further power-set step. EGH's
system size counts edges; taking its vertex set to be the union of the edges
gives the required vertex bound.

Corollary 12.5 obtains every uncountable chromatic cardinal by disjoint union
at limits. For a limit kappa the order is at most

  sup{beth_t(mu): aleph_0 <= mu < kappa}.

At an uncountable strong limit cardinal, including a singular strong limit,
this gives order exactly kappa in ZFC.

The GCH benchmark is expressly classical. EGH Corollary 2.2 gives the ZFC
lower bound |V(H)| >= mu^{+t} when chi(H)>mu in the bounded-intersection
class. Their own Corollary 12.3 and the same lower-colourability result already
give exact chi=mu+ at this order under GCH. The present manuscript does not
claim a new sharp GCH threshold. Its contribution here is the stated exact
ZFC calibration with an explicit proper colouring and its use in avoidance.

6. THE FULL UNCOUNTABLE AVOIDANCE SPECTRUM

Theorem C and Theorem 12.7 say that a finite r-uniform F has either no
uncountable chromatic avoidance cardinal or every one of them:

  Spec_r^unc(F) is empty                  if F belongs to B_r;
  Spec_r^unc(F) is all uncountable cardinals  otherwise.

For linear F, Theorem 12.2 applies. For nonlinear F, define

  tau(F)=max{|e intersect f|: e and f are distinct edges of F}.

Use intersection bound i=tau(F)-1 in the calibration theorem. At kappa=mu+,
this gives an F-free witness with order at most

  beth_{ceil((r-1)/(tau(F)-1))}(mu).

The witness omits F because an embedding preserves the shared vertices of
the selected pair of edges. The same intersection bound survives disjoint
union at limit cardinals. The bounds are sufficient host orders. They are
not asserted to be the minimum order of an arbitrary F-free witness.

7. WHY THE FAMILY THEOREM NEEDS LINEAR FORBIDDEN MEMBERS

Proposition 12.8 constructs, for every r >= 3, a finite family whose members
are individually non-obligatory but whose simultaneous omission forces
countable chromatic number. By Theorem 12.7 each individual member can even
be avoided at every prescribed uncountable cardinal.

One part of the family consists of all two-edge intersection types 2,...,r-1;
their omission forces a host to be linear. The other part consists of every
linear seven-edge r-uniform completion of the loose triple seven-cycle.
There are finitely many such completions. Each contains an odd Berge cycle,
so none belongs to B_r.

In a linear uncountably chromatic r-host, select one triple from each edge.
The triple system is still linear and has at least the original chromatic
number. Hajnal-Komjáth's linearly obligatory loose seven-cycle therefore
occurs in it. Restoring the seven original r-edges gives one of the prescribed
completions. The definition allows all identifications among added vertices
that preserve linearity; it does not assume that those vertices are private.

For triples this is the example appearing in Li's Section 7. The all-r
completion argument extends that obstruction. It does not claim a new
resolution of the original triple-system Problem 1177.

9. PUBLICATION FIT AND REMAINING REVIEW ISSUES

Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B is an ambitious first target if
the all-r classification and exact-cardinal proofs survive specialist review.
Its stated scope includes graph and hypergraph theory and asks for major
advances, solutions to open problems or new proof techniques. It explicitly
sets a higher standard for very long papers. At 57 pages, the introduction
must make Theorems B and C the reason for considering this full manuscript.
https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/journal-of-combinatorial-theory-series-b/0095-8956

European Journal of Combinatorics remains a credible alternative. Its stated
scope includes mathematical structures arising from combinatorial problems
and full-length research papers on important topics.
https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/european-journal-of-combinatorics/0195-6698

The likely referee questions are whether the hypergraph-base trace lemma
and reservoir calibration are fully justified; whether every infinite-cardinal
case, especially singular limits and disconnected forbidden systems, is
covered; and whether the higher-uniformity contribution is adequately
separated from Li and the classical EGH constructions. The new proofs and
introductory attribution address those questions directly. External review
and a specialist priority check remain appropriate before submission.

The finite factorization theory supplies consequences and structural depth.
It should support the main classification and spectrum contribution in the
submission rationale. Preserving the existing prose adds length, so a journal
editor may still request consolidation even if the mathematics is sound.
