2. THE UNCONDITIONAL CHARACTERIZATION

Theorem 13.1 (pages 54-55) fixes real coefficients lambda_ij, 1<=i<j<=r.
For every collection of finite probability spaces and nonnegative pair
kernels V_ij with both one-coordinate integrals equal to one, ask whether
there is a nonnegative joint density U whose pair marginals are

  M_ij = 1 + lambda_ij (V_ij-1).

This universal property holds if and only if lambda is in the matching
polytope of K_r. Equivalently:

  lambda_ij >= 0;
  sum_(j != i) lambda_ij <= 1 for every i;
  sum_({i,j} subset S) lambda_ij <= (|S|-1)/2 for every odd S.

It already suffices to test universality on a single common finite space
with one symmetric regular kernel repeated at every pair. Thus the criterion
handles different pair kernels and different spaces, while its obstructions
can be detected with two simple families of common test kernels.

Sufficiency mixes products over matchings. For necessity, fixed-point-free
involution kernels on large even alphabets force nonnegative coefficients
and the vertex constraints. Balanced binary antialignment forces every
odd-set inequality. Edmonds' classical theorem identifies the resulting
inequalities with the convex hull of matchings.

The symmetric scalar case recovers the existing transfer coefficient:

  lambda_r = 1/(r-1) when r is even, and 1/r when r is odd.

This is the largest coefficient that works for EVERY finite nonnegative
symmetric regular kernel. A particular kernel may admit larger values.
The joint kernel must be nonnegative: signed kernels would invalidate the
necessity statement. That word was checked explicitly during final review.

Why useful: the theorem decides whether a proposed graph-density witness
can be inserted into a genuine nonnegative joint construction. It also
shows the universal limits of that procedure. It is a full compatibility
criterion, rather than just another sufficient construction for one graph.

3. AN EXPLICIT TWO-SIDED DENSITY BOUND

Theorem 13.4 (pages 56-57) applies to every finite simple graph F with m>=1
edges, every r>=2, and every symmetric U:Omega^r->[0,1] of mean p:

  |t(F^[r],U)-p^m| <= m(m-1)p(1-p)/r.

There is no bipartiteness assumption. The same inequality is exact for
every finite simple r-uniform host G with p=p_r(G)=r!e(G)/|V(G)|^r.
It uses all homomorphisms, including noninjective maps; no unrecorded
sampling or injectivity error is hidden in this finite-host statement.

The proof is short and independent. Let M be the pair marginal and d the
one-coordinate marginal. Orthogonal singleton and pair projections give

  r||d-p||_2^2 + binom(r,2)||M-d(x)-d(y)+p||_2^2 <= p(1-p),

hence ||M-p||_2^2 <= 2p(1-p)/r. The first derivative of the graph-density
polynomial at the constant kernel vanishes. Its second derivative is bounded
by m(m-1)||M-p||_2^2. Integration of that derivative supplies the factor 1/2.

The exponent of r is optimal. For odd r, the majority kernel on balanced
signs has mean 1/2, and the two-edge path satisfies

  t(P3^[r],U)-1/4 ~ 1/(2*pi*r).

This is an analytic binomial identity followed by Stirling's formula, not
numerical evidence. Even an obligatory tree exhibits the full order 1/r.
The displayed coefficient m(m-1) is not asserted to be the best constant.

4. REGULARITY MAKES THE GIRTH DETERMINE THE RATE

Theorem 13.5 (pages 57-58) assumes each one-coordinate marginal of U is
exactly p. Forest expansions then have density exactly p^m. If F has girth g,

  |t(F^[r],U)-p^m|
    <= (2^m-1) [2p(1-p)/(r(r-1))]^(g/2).

Thus the error is O_F(r^(-g)), uniformly over the finite probability space,
the bounded kernel, and p. The same bound holds for finite regular hosts.

Regularity kills all terms with a degree-one vertex in the centered
edge-subset expansion. Each surviving term contains a cycle of length at
least g. A finite-dimensional self-adjoint operator estimate bounds that
cycle integral by the L2 norm of the centered marginal to its length.
The stronger regular projection bound then supplies the power r^(-g).

The order is optimal for EVERY fixed cyclic simple graph F, already at
exact density 1/2. With independent signs x_i and theta_r=r^(-1/2), use

  U_r(x) = 1/2 + [cos(theta_r sum_i x_i)-cos(theta_r)^r]/4.

It is symmetric, [0,1]-valued, and exactly regular. Its pair marginal is

  M_r(a,b)=1/2-b_r ab,
  b_r=(sin(theta_r)^2 cos(theta_r)^(r-2))/4
      ~ exp(-1/2)/(4r).

If N_g(F) counts the shortest cycles, sign averaging gives

  t(F^[r],U_r)-2^(-m)
    = N_g(F)(-b_r)^g 2^(-(m-g)) + O_F(b_r^(g+1)).

The coefficient is nonzero. For a cycle the formula is exactly (-b_r)^g.
This proves sharpness of the TWO-SIDED, absolute error rate. In a bipartite
graph the example has positive deviation; it is not claimed to attain a
negative Sidorenko deficit of that order.

For a canonical private-expansion atom, the core girth is its shortest
Berge-cycle length. This connects the quantitative estimate directly to
the cyclic structure studied in the classification.

6. RETAINED CONDITIONAL APPLICATION

Theorem D (page 4), Theorem 13.8 (pages 61-62), and Corollary 13.9 (page 62)
retain one fixed bipartite 2-connected graph J with 35 vertices and 66 edges.
For every r>=3 its private expansion A_r is one obligatory cyclic atom with

  e(A_r)=66,   v(A_r)=66r-97,   beta(A_r)=32.

Assuming the source rank-layer limit, this atom violates the Sidorenko
inequality in some finite simple r-host. The same graph has no Sidorenko
expansion for any r>=2, contradicting Nie-Spiro's published Conjecture 5.1
under that hypothesis. Corollary 13.10 (pages 62-64) retains the equality
example whose C4 expansion density differs from the random benchmark.

The source graph and finite-field construction are credited to OpenAI.
Our transfer uses the source family’s additional proper-edge-subgraph
limits, which are proved by repeating its coordinate expansion with
incidence-dependent activation laws. The rank-layer estimate remains the
imported hypothesis. The fixed uniformity is chosen BEFORE the field-size
limit; neither the witness nor its edge density is asserted independent of r.

Further internal reading of the long source proof found no concrete defect.
