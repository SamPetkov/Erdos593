REVISION ASSESSMENT: FROM CANONICAL ATOMS TO FINITE COUNTING
7 October 2026 — Complete 81-page author-review manuscript

1. WHAT THIS CONTINUATION CONTRIBUTES

The preceding version established an exact universal pair-marginal criterion
and quantitative bounds for private graph expansions. This revision develops
those results into a counting theory for every finite linear hypergraph,
including the full one-point assemblies described by the classification.

It also makes the arguments usable: explicit three- and four-coordinate
constructions, a stable finite-alphabet obstruction, exact star and cycle
examples, a degree-variance refinement, and a fully computed example in which
the location of an attachment changes the density while the atoms, attachment
multiplicities, vertex/edge counts, and cyclic rank stay fixed.

The additions connect the structural and quantitative parts of the paper.
They do not rely on the recent OpenAI rank-layer estimate. The older
classification/spectra proofs and conditional transfer proof are preserved.

2. THE FULL LINEAR-PATTERN THEOREM

Theorem 13.9 extends the density estimate to a finite simple linear r-uniform
pattern H with m>=1 edges, for any r>=2. For each edge e, let b_e count its
vertices that also belong to another edge, and put b=max_e b_e. Linearity gives
b<=min(r,m-1). If U is a symmetric [0,1]-valued r-kernel of mean p, then

  |t(H,U)-p^m|
    <= [p(1-p)/r] sum_{unordered edge pairs {e,f}} sqrt(b_e b_f)
    <= binom(m,2) [b/r] p(1-p).

The density counts all homomorphisms. The result is exact for the adjacency
kernel of every finite simple host; there is no hidden collision error in
this finite-host assertion.

The proof integrates vertices that occur in only one pattern edge. The
resulting factor on e is a b_e-coordinate marginal. The higher-coordinate
Hoeffding budget (Lemma 13.8) gives squared L2 norm at most
(b_e/r)p(1-p) after centering. A Taylor expansion around the constant p has
zero first derivative. Cauchy-Schwarz bounds each second derivative term,
and the integral remainder produces the displayed unordered-pair sum.

At fixed edge count m this gives a uniform error of order 1/r for all linear
incidence structures, not only individual expansion atoms. It includes
attachments through vertices that were private in the separate atoms.

3. REGULAR HOSTS, BERGE GIRTH, AND THE OBLIGATORY CLASS

If every one-coordinate marginal of U equals p, the higher projection budget
improves to b(b-1)p(1-p)/(r(r-1)). Theorem 13.9 gives exact equality
t(H,U)=p^m when H has no Berge cycle. If its Berge girth is g, it gives

  |t(H,U)-p^m|
    <= (2^m-1) [b(b-1)p(1-p)/(r(r-1))]^(g/2).

To prove this, expand in centered edge factors. Integrating private variables
annihilates a selected edge supported on zero or one shared point. Every
remaining term contains a Berge cycle. A shortest cycle in a linear pattern
has no extra intersections among its selected edges. Integrate variables
belonging to only one of these cycle factors, then bound the cyclic product
by the product of Hilbert-Schmidt norms. This gives the power r^(-g).

Corollary 13.11 applies the classification: an obligatory pattern is linear
and every Berge cycle is even. Its finite girth is therefore at least four.
For every fixed m, the entire obligatory class on m edges consequently has
error O_m(r^(-4)) in regular kernels and finite simple regular hosts. Berge
forests have exact equality. This statement concerns complete assemblies,
without assuming that their unrooted densities factor over the atoms.

4. OPTIMAL RATES FOR EVERY FIXED CYCLIC INCIDENCE PATTERN

Proposition 13.10 fixes the incidence data of shared vertices and adds private
vertices as r grows. The same regular cosine kernels as in the preceding
version have exact density 1/2:

  U_r(x)=1/2 + [cos(r^(-1/2) sum_i x_i)-cos(r^(-1/2))^r]/4,

on independent uniform signs. A j-coordinate Fourier coefficient, for even
j>=2, equals (1/4)(-1)^(j/2)sin(theta)^j cos(theta)^(r-j), independently of
the number of other retained coordinates. Its order is r^(-j/2).

Sign averaging selects the even-degree incidence subsets on both sides of
the Levi graph. The smallest nonempty such subsets are exactly its shortest
cycles, with 2g incidences. If N_g counts them and
b_r=(1/4)sin(theta)^2 cos(theta)^(r-2) ~ exp(-1/2)/(4r), then

  t(H_r,U_r)-2^(-m)
    = N_g(-b_r)^g 2^(-(m-g)) + O_H(r^(-g-1)).

The leading coefficient cannot cancel. Thus the r^(-g) order is optimal for
every fixed cyclic linear incidence pattern, including arbitrary canonical
assemblies. These are optimal ABSOLUTE ERROR rates. For obligatory patterns,
g is even and the leading deviation in this example is positive. The example
does not prove a negative Sidorenko deficit of this order, and the numerical
constants in the upper bounds are not claimed optimal.

5. DEGREE VARIANCE AND CYCLIC ERROR

Theorem 13.12 removes the need for exact regularity. Let d be the one-coordinate
degree function, put delta^2=Var(d), and define

  q^2 = [b(b-1)/(r(r-1))] [p(1-p)-r delta^2],
  C_{m,b} = [(b+1)(b+2)]^m.

The orthogonal projection budget makes the bracket nonnegative. The theorem
gives error at most C_{m,b} delta^2 for Berge forests, and at most
C_{m,b}(delta^2+q^g) for cyclic patterns of Berge girth g. Since b<=m-1,
this constant can be bounded using m alone. It is deliberately coarse.

Consequently, for a fixed incidence pattern,

  |t(H_r,U_r)-p_r^m| = O_H(Var(d_r)+r^(-g)).

The regular cyclic rate persists whenever the degree variance is O(r^(-g)).
For a finite host of order n, d(v)=(r-1)!deg(v)/n^(r-1), so this is an
explicit condition on its normalized degree sequence.

The proof decomposes every shared marginal into its constant, first-order
degree functions, and a higher remainder K. This remainder has zero marginals
on sets of size at most one; it is not assumed fully degenerate coordinate by
coordinate. Terms with at least two degree functions cost Var(d). With no
degree function, a surviving term contains a cycle. With exactly one degree
function, retain its marked point while integrating the other private
coordinates. A surviving incidence forest would have at most one possible
leaf, which is impossible. The remaining cycle terms cost delta q^(g/2),
absorbed by delta^2+q^g.

Small degree variance alone does not justify a universal leading coefficient
proportional to that variance: rare-event OR kernels can have a third degree
moment of the same order. The manuscript states the robust bound proved here,
without that stronger and unsupported asymptotic claim.

6. AN EXACT ATTACHMENT-SENSITIVE EXAMPLE

The rooted-gluing identity (19) writes a one-point density as the product
of the two unrooted densities plus the covariance of their rooted profiles.
Remark 13.13 turns this into an explicit example.

Take B=C4^[r]. Glue two copies either at two core vertices or at two private
vertices, giving H_c and H_p. Both have 8 edges, 8r-9 vertices, cyclic rank2,
the same two canonical atoms, and the same attachment excess profile (1).

On the uniform space {-1,0,1}, put s=E Z^2=2/3 and choose
0<epsilon<=min(p,1-p)/binom(r,2). The kernel

  U_r=p+epsilon sum_{i<j} Z_i Z_j

is [0,1]-valued, symmetric, and exactly regular. The atom's rooted densities
are a_c(z)=p^4+epsilon^4 s^3 z^2 and a_p(z)=p^4+epsilon^4 s^4, the latter
being constant. Therefore

  t(H_c,U_r)-t(H_p,U_r)
    = epsilon^8 s^6 Var(Z^2) = (128/6561)epsilon^8 > 0.

This distinguishes the position of a joint inside an atom from its
multiplicity. The direct statement is for bounded regular kernels. Ordinary
sampling preserves a strict density gap in finite simple hosts, but does not
automatically preserve exact regularity; that stronger realization is not
claimed for this three-point example.

7. USING THE MARGINAL CRITERION

The new construction subsection explains the sampling rule behind a matching
decomposition, then gives explicit formulas for r=3 and r=4. In four
coordinates, opposite-edge minima give the perfect-matching weights, the
residuals give singleton weights, and the empty-matching weight is one minus
the sum of the opposite-edge maxima. The degree and triangle constraints
ensure all weights are nonnegative.

Examples show why triangle inequalities and larger odd-set inequalities
cannot be removed. These are useful consequences of the preceding matching
polytope theorem, without a separate first-priority claim for the formulas.

Proposition 13.3 gives a stable finite obstruction. Write L=binom(r,2), take
lambda in [0,1]^L, and test its proposed marginals using the fixed-point-free
involution kernel V_N(x,y)=N 1[y=f(x)] on an even N-letter alphabet. If a
joint law realizes every target pair marginal within total variation eta,
then

  dist_infinity(lambda, matching_polytope) <= L/N+(L+1)eta.

Thus any profile at distance delta>0 has a finite exact obstruction for
every even N>L/delta, with an explicit lower bound on the unavoidable
marginal error. The estimate does not claim optimal constants.

8. MAKING THE CONDITIONAL TRANSFER'S ERROR BUDGET EXPLICIT

Before the retained transfer lemma, equation (20) now states that if the
full graph density is at most1-delta and every proper subgraph density is
within epsilon of1, then

  t(J,1+lambda(V-1))
    <=1-delta lambda^m+epsilon(1-lambda^m).

Hence epsilon(1-lambda^m)<delta lambda^m suffices for a strict deficit.
At the universal lambda_r~1/r, the retained signal has order r^(-m).
This explains the order of limits and the accuracy required of the proper
subgraph profile. It does not provide a uniform host density or field-size
bound. The graph/kernel notation and normalization are explicitly defined.

The existing OpenAI-based application retains its stated rank-layer
hypothesis. No new finite-field proof or Lean certification is asserted.

9. RELATION TO PRIOR WORK AND PUBLICATION ASSESSMENT

Hoeffding's decomposition, matching polytopes, cycle norm inequalities, and
rooted homomorphism identities are classical tools and are credited as such.
Kohayakawa-Nagle-Rodl-Schacht (JCTB100(2),2010,151-160), Theorem4, is now cited
for counting linear patterns under quasirandomness at fixed uniformity.
Nie-Spiro's published Theorem1.13 remains the acknowledged one-sided
high-uniformity predecessor for expansions. Their hypotheses and conclusions
differ from the uniform two-sided, regular Berge-girth, and degree-variance
statements proved here.

A targeted primary-source search did not locate these exact new statements.
This supports a carefully delimited novelty case and does not certify first
priority. The explicit calculations are explanatory examples and consequences;
they are not advertised individually as major independent discoveries.

The strongest coherent narrative is now: classify the obligatory finite
patterns; describe their canonical assembly; control the densities of every
such assembly using shared incidences, degree fluctuations, and Berge cycles;
then explain the separate conditional example that defeats an exact density
inequality. This gives the new analytic section a role in the main paper.

The larger manuscript still needs a clear editorial case for its length.
The new pages contain proofs, operational examples, and a generalization
beyond single atoms. They should be assessed for these contributions rather
than page count. The appropriate status remains an author-review research
draft. The prior ambitious JCTB positioning is not an acceptance prediction,
and internal checking is not external peer review.
