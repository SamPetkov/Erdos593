2. THE EXACT THEOREM

Theorem D (page 4), Theorem 13.4 and Corollary 13.5 (page 57) use a fixed
2-connected bipartite graph J with 35 vertices and 66 edges. For every r >= 3,
its private r-expansion A_r has

  e(A_r) = 66,
  v(A_r) = 35 + 66(r-2) = 66r-97,
  beta(A_r) = (r-1)66-v(A_r)+1 = 32.

It is a single canonical cyclic atom. In particular, for triples the example
has 101 vertices and 66 edges. It is linear, every Berge cycle is even, and
each hyperedge has exactly r-2 private leaves in its Levi graph.

Nevertheless, conditional on the imported rank-layer estimate, some finite
simple r-uniform host G satisfies

  t(A_r,G) < p_r(G)^66,

where t(F,G) counts all edge-preserving maps divided by |V(G)|^|V(F)| and
p_r(G)=r!e(G)/|V(G)|^r. Thus the inequality has its ordinary homomorphism
meaning, without prescribed vertex types or an injectivity restriction.

Obligatoriness is a different assertion: every uncountably chromatic host
contains an injective non-induced copy. Reiher's positive theorem already
establishes that assertion for private bipartite expansions. The manuscript's
canonical structure identifies this specific expansion as one atom.

3. WHY THE TRANSFER IS NOT AUTOMATIC

For any symmetric r-variable kernel U, let M_U be its two-variable marginal.
Private vertices integrate independently, giving the exact identity

  t(J^[r],U) = t(J,M_U),       mean(U) = mean(M_U).

It follows that a Sidorenko graph has Sidorenko expansions. That is the
known forward implication in Nie-Spiro's published Corollary 1.14.
An arbitrary nonnegative graph kernel need not be the pair marginal of a
symmetric nonnegative r-kernel. A graph counterexample therefore cannot be
inserted into this identity without constructing a realizable marginal.

The new Lemma 13.1 performs that construction. For a symmetric nonnegative
graph kernel V with every row integral equal to one, average products of V
over all maximum matchings on r positions. The result U_r is symmetric,
nonnegative and has mean one. Its pair marginal is exactly

  M_(U_r) = 1 + lambda_r (V-1),
  lambda_r = 1/(r-1) for even r, and 1/r for odd r.

The source witness has a second useful property. In its fixed family V_q,
all proper spanning edge-subgraphs J_B have density tending to one, while
the full J has limiting density a < 1. Consequently

  t(J, 1+lambda_r(V_q-1)) -> 1 + lambda_r^66(a-1) < 1.

This is enough for each fixed r. The graph J is common to all uniformities,
but the prime q and witness may depend on r. One fixed finite kernel is not
asserted to work for arbitrarily small lambda_r.

Scaling U_r into [0,1] preserves the strict homogeneous comparison. Sampling
independent vertex labels and then independent r-edges produces an ordinary
finite simple hypergraph witness. The proof controls every noninjective map
by the collision bound and keeps the falling-factorial density correction.

4. THE ADDITIONAL SOURCE PROPERTY IS PROVED HERE

The external manuscript does not state the proper-edge-subgraph limit as
one of its conclusions. Proposition 13.3 extracts it from the actual
coordinate-expansion proof.

For a proper edge subset B of the source incidence graph, force the
activation at an omitted source incidence to be empty. The local zero-moment
identities make both adjacent point-pair labels empty; emptiness propagates
through the connected face-neighbor graph. Only the all-empty label term
survives, with limiting value one.

There is a scope detail in this step: when vertex types repeat, deleting one
source incidence makes the activation law depend on the incidence, rather
than only on its type pair. The proof repeats the coordinate expansion with
these laws. It uses the source rank-layer lemma's quantification over every
active incidence subset and sign choice, not a substitution into a narrower
stated lemma. Isolated vertices integrate to one. Both rectangular
orientations are treated, and the crossed symmetric product factors their
densities. Exact constant row and column integrals follow from translation
invariance of the normalized rank-layer kernels.

These are mathematical deductions from the pinned source. They do not
replace the source's substantial finite-field proof.

5. A SECOND DENSITY CONSEQUENCE

Corollary 13.6 (pages 58-59) keeps a positive density fixed. For every fixed
r >= 3 there are p in (0,1) and finite simple r-hosts G_n such that

  p_r(G_n) -> p,
  t(A_r,G_n) -> p^66,
  t(C_4^[r],G_n) -> q > p^4.

The proof interpolates on independent coordinate spaces between the negative
kernel and a positive sign perturbation. The pair marginal remains
nonconstant throughout the interpolation; the finite-kernel C4 equality
case forces a strict excess. Sampling realizes all three limits together.

Both A_r and C_4^[r] are obligatory patterns. Thus matching the first atom's
random density does not determine the other's. The explicit three limits
avoid ambiguities among hypergraph quasirandomness definitions. The result
is at one positive density for each r, not at every density.
