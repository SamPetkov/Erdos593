REVISION NOTE: THE HIGHER-UNIFORMITY EXTENSION
6 October 2026

1. WHAT HAS BEEN ADDED

The manuscript now contains a complete ordinary mathematical proof of a
classification for every fixed finite uniformity r >= 3. Its existing triple
classification and finite development remain intact in Sections 1-10. The
extension is stated as Theorem B in the introduction and proved as Theorem 11.1.

For a finite simple r-uniform hypergraph F, the following are equivalent:

  (i) F embeds into every r-uniform hypergraph of uncountable chromatic number.

  (ii) F is generated from private r-uniform expansions of finite bipartite
       graphs and finite edgeless systems by finite disjoint unions and
       one-point amalgamations.

  (iii) After isolated vertices are removed, F is linear, every hyperedge-node
        of its Levi graph has at least r-2 incident bridges, and every Berge
        cycle is even.

Embeddings are injective and non-induced. Chromatic number uses proper vertex
colourings with no monochromatic edge. The host has the same uniformity as F.
A bridge incidence is not required to terminate at a point of global degree one.

The canonical atoms are single r-edges and private r-uniform expansions of
finite simple 2-connected bipartite graphs. This is Corollary 11.7.

2. THE NEW PROOF MECHANISM

Lemma 11.3 extends the one-apex sequence lift to a k-uniform base hypergraph H.
The lift is (k+1)-uniform and preserves uncountable chromatic number. Every
finite linear trace decomposes into pieces D_s^+, where D_s is a finite linear
subhypergraph of H and the superscript adds one private vertex per edge.

Three details support the induction. A base edge cannot be used twice in a
linear trace because its k >= 2 vertices would be shared. Distinct bases in a
single fibre have distinct apices because they prescribe different next branch
symbols. The fibre/shared-point incidence graph is a forest: at a shortest
source node on a hypothetical cycle, the two neighbours would lie in different
immediate branches, while the remaining path cannot change that branch.

Lemma 11.4 then shows that inflation commutes with one-point assembly. This
includes attachment at a point that was private in an earlier expansion. At
each inflation step only the newly added private vertices must remain distinct.

Starting with a graph G and iterating the lift gives r-uniform hosts H_r(G).
Proposition 11.5 shows that every finite linear trace K in H_r(G) is a forest
assembly of J^[r], with J a finite subgraph of G and |E(J)| <= |E(K)|.

For a finite forbidden system F with m edges, take G of uncountable chromatic
number without odd cycles of length at most m. All graph constituents arising
from an m-edge trace are then bipartite. Thus an embedding of a linear F
outside the claimed class would be impossible. The classical nonlinear
obstruction deals with nonlinear F. Reiher's positive theorem and the known
closure operations establish the other implication.

The witness depends on m. The argument does not require a single host all of
whose finite traces are obligatory. Intermediate hosts need not be linear;
linearity is retained for the finite projected traces where the induction uses it.

3. WHY THE HIGHER BRIDGE THRESHOLD MATTERS

Remark 11.8 gives a five-edge 4-uniform system:

  e = {a1,a2,a3,p}, f = {b1,b2,b3,q},
  g_i = {a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i}, i = 1,2,3,

with all named vertices distinct except for the displayed repetitions. It is
linear and all its Berge cycles have length four. Every edge has a bridge
incidence, but e and f have only one each. The four-uniform criterion requires
two, so the system is non-obligatory. Merely carrying over the triple threshold
of one bridge would give an incorrect characterization.

4. FINITE CONSEQUENCES

For a reduced nonempty obligatory r-uniform hypergraph, put

  n = |V(F)|, m = |E(F)|, c = number of components,
  s = n-(r-2)m, beta = (r-1)m-n+c.

Corollary 11.9 proves that at prescribed m >= 1 and 1 <= c <= m, the possible
orders n are exactly the integers in

  (r-2)m + 2(c-1) + ceil(2 sqrt(m-c+1)) <= n <= (r-1)m+c.

The atom-count spectra, connected indecomposability phase diagram, canonical
factorization products, fixed-atom attachment profiles, cyclic budget, and
piece-count bounds transfer with the stated parameter change and the original
hypotheses. The proof uses additive counts of the ordinary graph cores. It does
not assume that arbitrary attachment ports on a larger-uniformity atom can be
copied vertex for vertex into a triple-system atom. Fixed-atom profile realization
uses the existing construction requiring at most two distinct ports per atom.

These are exact FINITE structural spectra. The revision does not claim exact
chromatic-cardinal avoidance spectra in higher uniformities or a new resolution
of Erdős Problem 1177.

5. LITERATURE POSITIONING

Li's currently checked arXiv v2, dated 23 July 2026, states the classification
and exact chromatic-cardinal results for triple systems. Its graph-based
one-apex construction is closely related to the lift in the existing manuscript.
The original acknowledgement of Li's earlier triple classification is retained.
Source: Eric Li, A Resolution of Erdős Problems 593 and 1177: Obligatory Triple
Systems and Exact Spectra, arXiv:2606.24882v2.
https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24882v2

Reiher already proves that private expansions of finite complete bipartite
graphs are obligatory in every finite uniformity. His introduction also records
the applicable closure properties and nonlinear obstruction. Thus the positive
all-uniformity direction is existing mathematics and is explicitly credited.
Source: Christian Reiher, Obligatory hypergraphs, Theorem 1.2 and Section 1.
https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11223v1
https://doi.org/10.1090/proc/17021

The all-uniformity nonlinear obstruction is the consequence of Theorem 2 in
Erdős, Hajnal and Rothschild, On chromatic number of graphs and set systems
(1973), p. 532. The starting graph with large odd girth is the same
Erdős-Hajnal input already used in the original manuscript.
https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1973-13.pdf
https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1966-07.pdf

The extension is stronger than the classification statements in those primary
sources. The targeted search did not locate an existing full all-uniformity
classification. That bounded search is not a proof of first priority. The precise
contribution to emphasize is the hypergraph-base finite linear trace lemma,
its iteration, and the resulting classification with r-2 bridge incidences.

7. PUBLICATION ASSESSMENT

My assessment is that the broader classification substantially improves the
paper's publication case relative to a new presentation of the triple theorem
with finite corollaries. This assessment depends on the new proof and its
priority surviving ordinary specialist scrutiny; it is not an acceptance forecast.

Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B is an ambitious first target worth
considering. Its stated scope emphasizes graph and hypergraph theory and major
structural advances. Its policy also explicitly imposes higher standards on very
long papers, so the 50-page length is a material editorial consideration. The
introduction now makes the extension visible as Theorem B; the full earlier
development is retained in accordance with the author's preference for its prose.
https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/journal-of-combinatorial-theory-series-b/0095-8956

European Journal of Combinatorics is a credible alternative given its stated
interest in mathematical structures arising from combinatorial problems and
full-length research papers on important topics.
https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/european-journal-of-combinatorics/0195-6698

The strongest accurate description is: "We extend the classification of
obligatory triple systems to every finite uniformity by iterating a finite
linear trace lemma over hypergraph bases, and obtain the corresponding canonical
atoms and exact finite structural spectra."
