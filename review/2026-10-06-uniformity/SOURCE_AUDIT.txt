ADDITIONAL C4 PROFILE CHECK

## 8. C4 compatibility and necessary growth of the normalized witnesses

The proper-subgraph profile includes a C4, so the crossed regular kernels satisfy t(C4,V_q) -> 1. This does not by itself contradict t(J,V_q) -> a < 1: the kernels are nonnegative and 1-regular but are not bounded uniformly in q. The following elementary estimate makes the issue precise.

For every finite symmetric nonnegative 1-regular kernel V, let B=max(1,||V||_infinity), and let F be any finite simple graph with m>=1 edges. Then

    |t(F,V)-1| <= m B^(m-1) [t(C4,V)-1]^(1/4).

Proof. Set D=V-1, let P be projection onto constants, and let T_D be its kernel operator. Regularity gives PT_D=T_DP=0, hence T_V=P+T_D and

    t(C4,V)-1 = trace(T_D^4) >= ||T_D||_op^4.

Telescope t(F,V)-t(F,1) across its m edges. In a term indexed by the edge uv, fix all variables except x_u,x_v. As F is simple, the remaining factors split into a constant factor and a function of x_u times a function of x_v. Their product of sup-norm bounds is at most B^(m-1), and the operator-norm inequality bounds this term by B^(m-1)||T_D||_op. Integrate the fixed variables and sum the m terms.

Consequently, if delta_q=t(C4,V_q)-1 tends to zero while t(J,V_q) tends to a<1, then for all sufficiently large q,

    ||V_q||_infinity >= [(1-a)/(2m)]^(1/(m-1)) delta_q^(-1/(4(m-1))),

where m=66. In particular normalized witnesses must become unbounded. No contradiction arises merely from the proper C4 limit. A bounded-kernel quasirandomness argument would require a hypothesis that the source construction does not satisfy.

The independent unconditional extensions discovered during this audit are written separately in substantive_extension_candidates.tex: universal pair-marginal profiles are exactly Edmonds' matching polytope; bounded symmetric r-kernels satisfy a sharp-order two-sided O_F(1/r) expansion-density bound; and exactly regular kernels satisfy a sharp-order O_F(r^(-girth(F))) bound for every cyclic F. These results do not use the imported rank-layer lemma.

# Independent audit of the Family 161 transfer to obligatory atoms

## Verdict and source boundary

The proposed transfer is mathematically sound, conditional on the rank-layer limit in the pinned Family 161 manuscript. This audit read the full local `family161/kernel.tex`, `complex.tex`, `introduction.tex`, and `consequences.tex`. It independently checked the deductions from that limit; it does not claim to have independently re-proved the source's finite-field transverse and singular-tail estimates.

The outcome is substantive for E593: in every finite uniformity r >= 3 there is a SINGLE canonical cyclic obligatory atom with 66 edges which fails the random-density homomorphism lower bound. The following checks resolve the main possible transfer gaps.

## 1. The source graph is 2-connected

Let H be the point-face incidence graph of the source complex. Every pair lies in exactly two faces, the face-neighbour graph is connected, and every point belongs to a face. Thus H is connected.

Delete a point-node i. Each pair shared by two adjacent faces still contains at least one other point, so every path in the face-neighbour graph lifts to a walk connecting its face-nodes in H-i. Every remaining point-node still attaches to a face. Hence H-i is connected.

Delete a face-node j={a,b,c}. For each of its pairs ab, ac, bc, the other face on that pair gives a two-edge path between its endpoints avoiding j. All three neighbours of j therefore remain connected. Since H was connected, every component of H-j contains a neighbour of j, and H-j is connected.

Consequently H is a simple 2-connected bipartite graph. As a supplementary finite check, parsing ONLY the first face table gives 22 distinct faces, 33 pairs each of multiplicity two, 35 incidence vertices, 66 incidence edges, and connectedness after every single-vertex deletion. The written proof above is independent of that computation.

For its r-private expansion F_r=H^[r], the existing canonical-atom theorem therefore gives one cyclic atom, with

    e(F_r)=66,  v(F_r)=35+66(r-2)=66r-97,
    beta(F_r)=66-35+1=32.

## 2. Exact regularity of the source kernels

For each fixed x=(a,(x^k)), integrating W_q(x,y) in y gives one EXACTLY, not just asymptotically. Conditional on each y-type and each sampled activation, every difference x^k-y^k is uniform, the active coordinates are independent, and each rank-layer kernel has integral one. Averaging the activation and type laws preserves one. The analogous argument integrating x for fixed y also gives one. Thinning and the inactive dilution type preserve both identities.

For the crossed kernel

    V_q((x,y),(x',y'))=W_q(x,y') W_q(x',y),

the integral in (x',y') is consequently the product of two ones. Thus V_q is symmetric, nonnegative, and exactly 1-regular on its finite probability space. In particular its mean is exactly one.

## 3. Every proper spanning edge-subgraph has density tending to one

Keep all finite construction parameters, including the dilution, fixed as q tends to infinity. Let A be any proper subset of the 66 source incidences, and write H_A for the spanning graph with this edge set. In the expansion of its rectangular kernel density, give every omitted source incidence the deterministic empty activation.

At an omitted corner the local factor is one only when both adjacent pair labels are empty, and is zero otherwise. At all retained corners the zero-moment condition still forbids one empty and one nonempty adjacent label. Thus all three pair labels on the affected face are empty. Connected face adjacency propagates emptiness to every face and every pair. Only the all-empty label assignment survives, with contribution one.

The source rank-layer lemma quantifies over every active incidence subset B, uniformly over the fixed finite family of signs. It therefore applies to this expansion. The finite sum/product error argument in the coordinate-expansion proof gives

    t_(I,J)(H_A,W_q) -> 1,
    t_(I,J)(H_A,W_q^top) -> 1.

Isolated vertices of H_A cause no difficulty: their sampled types and matrices integrate to one. Repeated types also cause no difficulty: distinct source vertices still have independent matrix samples, and distinct source incidences have independent activation samples conditional on types.

PROOF-WRITING CAVEAT: the displayed coordinate-expansion lemma is formulated with a law depending only on a type pair. Deleting one source incidence can make the law incidence-dependent when type pairs repeat. Repeat its finite expansion proof with the omitted incidences forced inactive; do not call this a literal substitution into its narrower statement. This costs only one paragraph and avoids a real scope mismatch.

The crossed product factors in the two independent coordinate orientations, so t(H_A,V_q)->1 for every proper A. For the full H, the source construction gives t(H,V_q)->a<1. The limit is nonnegative, as each density is nonnegative.

For every fixed 0<lambda<=1, expand the nonnegative mixture rather than centered products:

    t(H,(1-lambda)+lambda V_q)
      = sum_(A subset E(H)) lambda^|A| (1-lambda)^(66-|A|) t(H_A,V_q)
      -> 1 + lambda^66 (a-1) < 1.

Choose q only AFTER fixing lambda (and hence after fixing r below). Uniformity in unbounded r or a single q serving every r is neither required nor asserted.

## 4. Maximum-matching symmetrization

For any symmetric nonnegative finite 1-regular kernel V, average

    R_r(z_1,...,z_r)=average_M product_(ab in M) V(z_a,z_b)

over all maximum matchings of the complete graph on [r]. Permutations of [r] permute this family, so R_r is symmetric. Every matching product has mean one because its pairs have disjoint vertex variables, so R_r has mean one.

Integrate out all but z_1,z_2. If 12 is a matching edge, the marginal is V(z_1,z_2). Otherwise each incident partner integrates away to one by exact 1-regularity, and every remaining pair integrates to one. The marginal is therefore

    Q_r=(1-lambda_r)+lambda_r V,
    lambda_r=1/(r-1) for even r, and lambda_r=1/r for odd r.

These probabilities also follow from dividing floor(r/2) by binom(r,2). They are strictly positive for every fixed r>=3.

Each of the r-2 private vertices on an expansion edge occurs on that edge alone. Fubini therefore gives the EXACT identity

    t(H^[r],R_r)=t(H,Q_r).

With V=V_q and sufficiently large q, this is less than one, whereas the mean of R_r is one. The kernel is finite and bounded; scaling by its maximum L produces U_r in [0,1], with mean p=1/L and

    t(F_r,U_r)<p^66.

Strictness forces L>1, so 0<p<1. No graph-kernel Sidorenko inequality is being applied to an arbitrary pair marginal without constructing a symmetric r-kernel that realizes it: the matching average explicitly supplies that realization.

## 5. Transfer to finite simple r-uniform hosts

Use the all-homomorphisms convention

    t(F,G)=Hom(F,G)/N^v(F),
    p_r(G)=r! e(G)/N^r=t(single r-edge,G).

Sample N independent vertex labels from U_r's finite probability space and, conditional on them, sample each unordered r-set independently with its U_r probability. This produces a finite SIMPLE r-uniform host.

Put v=66r-97, tau=t(F_r,U_r), and Delta=p^66-tau>0. Every globally injective map of F_r has homomorphism probability exactly tau. The proportion of noninjective maps is at most binom(v,2)/N, so

    E t(F_r,G_N) <= tau + binom(v,2)/N.

No independence for noninjective maps is assumed. The edge-density expectation is exactly

    E p_r(G_N) = ((N)_r/N^r) p.

Since (N)_r/N^r >= 1-binom(r,2)/N, Bernoulli's inequality and Jensen give

    (E p_r(G_N))^66 >= p^66(1-66 binom(r,2)/N),
    E[p_r(G_N)^66] >= (E p_r(G_N))^66.

Thus any integer N>=v satisfying

    N > [binom(v,2)+66 binom(r,2) p^66]/Delta

has some realization with t(F_r,G_N)<p_r(G_N)^66. Such a realization has an edge. Ordinary density e(G_N)/binom(N,r) differs from p_r by the usual falling-factorial correction; do not silently identify the two at finite N.

## 6. Optional explicit density-forcing separation

This extra consequence is also sound, but an explicit formulation avoids ambiguity among hypergraph quasirandomness conventions.

Take U_- above of mean p in (0,1), with symmetric pair marginal Q_-. This marginal is nonconstant, since t(H,Q_-)<p^66. On independent balanced signs define

    U_+(a_1,...,a_r)=p+delta sum_(i<j) a_i a_j,
    0<delta<=min(p,1-p)/binom(r,2).

It is symmetric, [0,1]-valued, and of mean p. Its pair marginal is p+delta ab. Expanding t(H,p+delta ab) leaves precisely the even-degree edge subsets, all with nonnegative coefficients. H contains a cycle, so this density is strictly larger than p^66.

On the product vertex space interpolate U_s=(1-s)U_-+sU_+. All kernels remain symmetric, [0,1]-valued, and of mean p. Their pair marginals are Q_s=(1-s)Q_-+s(p+delta ab). The two centered terms depend on independent coordinate pairs, so

    ||Q_s-p||_2^2=(1-s)^2||Q_--p||_2^2+s^2 delta^2>0.

Continuity supplies s* in (0,1) with t(F_r,U_s*)=p^66. The nonconstant symmetric pair marginal on a finite positive-atom space satisfies t(C4,Q_s*)>p^4 by the source's two-Cauchy--Schwarz equality argument. Hence

    t(C4^[r],U_s*)>p^4.

Sampling and choosing deterministic realizations gives finite simple r-hosts of increasing orders with

    p_r(G_N)->p,
    t(F_r,G_N)->p^66,
    t(C4^[r],G_N)->q>p^4.

For each fixed test the expectation error is O(1/N). Indicators for two maps with disjoint images are independent; overlapping pairs constitute O(1/N), giving variance O(1/N). Chebyshev and a union bound for the three tests justify simultaneous convergence. This is a concrete failure of these two densities to determine all random-hypergraph densities. It does not need a claim about a named hypergraph forcing conjecture.

7. Mathematical scope of the transfer and bounded novelty conclusion
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The matching average realizes the pair marginal 1+lambda_r(V-1), where
lambda_r = 1/(r-1) for even r and 1/r for odd r. Proper-subgraph limits
give the limiting deficit (1-a)lambda_r^66, strictly positive for every
fixed finite r. The finite q may depend on r; no uniform q for all
unbounded r is asserted. Nonnegative finite kernels may be normalized
into [0,1], and ordinary sampling produces finite simple hosts.

This does not mean arbitrary graph counterexamples lift, nor that
non-Sidorenko behavior is monotone under taking supergraphs. It also
does not show all proper subgraphs of J are Sidorenko: the equalities
hold only along this particular witness sequence.

The positive obligatory assertion for private bipartite expansions
already follows from Reiher's earlier theorem. Our intrinsic structure
theory additionally identifies the expansions as individual canonical
atoms and records their bridge profile, but should not absorb credit
for the earlier positive inclusion.
