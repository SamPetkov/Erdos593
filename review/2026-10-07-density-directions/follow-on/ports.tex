\subsection{A rooted sufficient condition and the limits of attachment data}

The full port profiles retain information that the atom list loses.
They also give a useful sufficient condition for an exact density lower
bound.  We state it for actual finite assemblies, rather than assume
that their unrooted densities multiply.  Throughout this subsection,
$\mu$ is a probability law on a finite set $\Omega$, and $U:\Omega^r\to[0,1]$
is symmetric, with $r\ge3$ and $p=\mathbb EU$.  We call $U$ regular if
every one-coordinate marginal equals $p$ on the support of $\mu$.
Zero-mass states can be removed without changing any density.

For an actual vertex $a$ of a piece $A$, write $\phi_{A,a}(x)$ for
its homomorphism density with $a$ fixed at $x$.  Partition the actual
edge set of $H$ among pieces, and let a joint be a vertex in at least
two piece supports.  Suppose the bipartite piece--joint incidence
graph is a forest, with each component rooted at a piece.  Thus each
nonroot piece has one actual parent joint.  These hypotheses include
the original vertex and edge identifications; they are not consequences
of a formal product of unrelated kernels.

\begin{lemma}[A root-safe forest criterion]
\label{followon-root-safe-forest}
Let $m_i$ be the number of edges of piece $A_i$.  If every nonroot
piece satisfies $\phi_{A_i,a_i}(x)\ge p^{m_i}$ at its parent joint
$a_i$, for every $x$ in the support of $\mu$, and every root piece
satisfies $t(A_i,U)\ge p^{m_i}$, then
\[
 t(H,U)\ge p^{|E(H)|}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Remove pieces in decreasing distance from the root.  After their
descendants have been removed, a nonroot piece has only its parent
coordinate shared with the remaining assembly.  Integrating its other
vertices gives its rooted profile, multiplied by the constants already
obtained from its descendants.  All remaining factors are nonnegative,
so that profile can be replaced by $p^{m_i}$.  At a joint of higher
multiplicity the child profiles use the same coordinate; its probability
weight is kept once, not once per child.  The root bound finishes each
component.  Components multiply, and isolated vertices contribute one.
The edge partition gives $\sum_i m_i=|E(H)|$.
\end{proof}

The local hypothesis is stronger than the unrooted Sidorenko
inequality.  For even-cycle expansions, it can be proved at their core
vertices.  This identifies a geometric subclass in which assembly
preserves the lower bound for regular kernels.

\begin{proposition}[Core-admissible cycle assemblies]
\label{followon-core-admissible-cycles}
Suppose every piece is a single $r$-edge or a private expansion
$C_{2k}^{[r]}$, with $k\ge2$.  If the piece--joint forest admits roots
such that the parent joint of every nonroot cycle piece is a core
vertex of that piece, then every regular $U$ satisfies
\[
 t(H,U)\ge p^{|E(H)|}.
\]
The corresponding vertex in its parent piece may be core or private.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $M$ be the pair marginal of $U$, and let $K_j(x,y)$ be the
weighted density of a $j$-edge path in $M$ with its endpoints fixed.
Regularity gives $\mathbb E_yM(x,y)=p$ and, inductively,
$\mathbb E_yK_j(x,y)=p^j$.  Symmetry gives $K_j(x,y)=K_j(y,x)$.
After the private coordinates of a $2k$-cycle are integrated, its
profile at a core vertex $x$ is
\[
 \phi_{\rm c}(x)
 =\mathbb E_yK_k(x,y)^2
 \ge\bigl(\mathbb E_yK_k(x,y)\bigr)^2
 =p^{2k},
\]
by weighted Cauchy--Schwarz.  Averaging proves the root-piece bound.
For a single edge the rooted profile is $p$ at every port.
Apply Lemma~\ref{followon-root-safe-forest}.  The argument uses no
division, so it also covers $p=0$.
\end{proof}

This is an exact regular-kernel bound, not the full Sidorenko property.
The proof uses classical Cauchy--Schwarz and leaf integration.
Conditional-independence gluing and stronger restricted-density
conditions have a substantial earlier theory: see the smoothness
calculus of Li--Szegedy~\citep{liszegedy2011} and the hypergraph
coupling and reflection criteria of Szegedy~\citep{szegedy2014}.
No universal covariance sign is used here.

There is a second positive case without the core-port restriction.
Take copies of one fixed even-cycle expansion and identify one chosen
vertex from each copy at a single joint.  Let $a$ copies use a core
vertex and $b$ use a private vertex.  The automorphisms of the expansion
make their respective profiles $c$ and $\ell$ independent of which
vertex in each class was chosen.  Regularity and the preceding proof
give $c\ge p^{2k}$ pointwise and $\mathbb E\ell=t(C_{2k}^{[r]},U)
\ge p^{2k}$.  Thus
\[
 \mathbb E c^a\ell^b
 \ge p^{2ka}\mathbb E\ell^b
 \ge p^{2ka}(\mathbb E\ell)^b
 \ge p^{2k(a+b)},
\]
with the usual empty-product convention when $b=0$.
The middle inequality is Jensen.  This proves the lower bound for
all port choices at this one joint; it says nothing about correlations
at two different joints of an atom.

\subsubsection{The same atom can have negatively correlated ports}

The preceding core-port condition cannot be replaced by a pointwise
statement about every port, even for an even-cycle atom.  There is also
no uniform density ordering between core--core and private--private
attachment.  The following exact witness complements the ternary
example already in the manuscript.

\begin{proposition}[Negative correlation and attachment incomparability]
\label{followon-negative-root-correlation}
For every $r\ge3$, let $B_r=C_4^{[r]}$.  There is a bounded regular
symmetric $r$-kernel for which its core and private rooted profiles
have negative covariance.  If $H_{\rm cc}$ and $H_{\rm pp}$ identify
two otherwise disjoint copies of $B_r$ at core--core and
private--private roots, respectively, neither pattern dominates the
other in homomorphism density over all regular symmetric $r$-kernels.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First take $r=3$.  Give $\Omega=\{1,-1/2\}$ the masses $1/3,2/3$, and put
\[
 p=\frac12,\qquad a=\frac1{20},\qquad b=-\frac1{10},\qquad
 U(x,y,z)=p+a(xy+xz+yz)+bxyz.
\]
The kernel has value $7/16$ when exactly one coordinate is positive
and $11/20$ otherwise.  It is symmetric and bounded.
Writing $s=\mathbb EZ^2=1/2$, we have $\mathbb EZ=0$ and
$\mathbb EZ^3=1/4$.  Averaging any two coordinates of $U$ leaves
$p$, so the kernel is regular.  Its pair marginal is $M(x,y)=p+axy$.
The $j$-edge path kernel is
\[
 K_j(x,y)=p^j+a^js^{j-1}xy\qquad(j\ge1),
\]
as follows by induction and centring.

Let $c(z)$ and $\ell(z)$ be the core and private profiles of $B_3$.
The core profile is $K_4(z,z)$; at a private root the three other
edges give the path kernel $K_3$.  Hence
\[
 c(z)=p^4+a^4s^3z^2,\qquad
 q:=t(B_3,U)=p^4+a^4s^4,\qquad
 \ell(z)=\mathbb E_{x,y}U(x,y,z)K_3(x,y)
        =q+ba^3s^4z.
\]
Set $\varepsilon=1/2560000$.  Since $z^2=(1+z)/2$ on this space,
these formulas become
\[
 q=\frac1{16}+\varepsilon,\qquad
 c(z)=q+\varepsilon z,\qquad
 \ell(z)=q-2\varepsilon z.
\]
In particular $\ell(1)=p^4-\varepsilon<p^4$, and
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(c(Z),\ell(Z))=-\varepsilon^2<0.
\]
If $H_{\rm cp}$ identifies a core root with a private root, the
three densities are exactly
\[
 \begin{split}
 t(H_{\rm cc},U)&=q^2+\varepsilon^2/2,\\
 t(H_{\rm pp},U)&=q^2+2\varepsilon^2,\\
 t(H_{\rm cp},U)&=q^2-\varepsilon^2
                 =\frac1{256}+\frac1{20480000}.
 \end{split}
\]
Thus $H_{\rm pp}$ has larger density than $H_{\rm cc}$ here.
All three still exceed the benchmark $p^8$.

To extend the witness to every $r\ge3$, keep the same two-point law and set
\[
 a_r=\frac1{4\bigl(\binom r2+2\binom r3\bigr)},\qquad
 U_r(z_1,\ldots,z_r)
 =\frac12+a_r\sum_{i<j}z_i z_j
             -2a_r\sum_{i<j<k}z_i z_j z_k.
\]
Since $|z_i|\le1$, the kernel lies in $[1/4,3/4]$.  Centring gives
regularity.  Its two- and three-coordinate marginals are the preceding
formulas with $a=a_r$ and $b=-2a_r$.  Thus, with
$\varepsilon_r=a_r^4/16$ and $q_r=p^4+\varepsilon_r$, the two profiles
are still $q_r+\varepsilon_rz$ and $q_r-2\varepsilon_rz$.
All the strict correlation and ordering conclusions follow.  At $r=3$
this is exactly the displayed rational witness.

For the opposite ordering at every uniformity, use the manuscript's
uniform space $\{-1,0,1\}$ and
$V_r=1/2+\eta\sum_{i<j}z_i z_j$, where
$0<\eta\le1/(2\binom r2)$.  Here $s=2/3$, the private profile is
constant, and the core profile is $p^4+\eta^4s^3z^2$.  Consequently
\[
 t(H_{\rm cc},V_r)-t(H_{\rm pp},V_r)
 =\eta^8s^6\operatorname{Var}(Z^2)
 =\frac{128}{6561}\eta^8>0.
\]
This proves both strict directions.
\end{proof}

At uniformity $r$, each of these assemblies has the same two canonical
cycle atoms, eight edges, $8r-9$ vertices, Levi cycle rank two and one joint of
atom multiplicity two.  Thus that structural data does not even
determine a host-independent density ordering.  In particular,
copies of one Sidorenko atom need not have positively correlated
profiles at different port orbits.  This witness refutes that sign
shortcut, not Sidorenko assembly closure: its negative covariance
is smaller than its positive atom-density excess.

\subsubsection{The remaining attachment question}

\begin{conjecture}[Regular cycle-assembly closure]
\label{followon-regular-cycle-assembly-question}
For every $r\ge3$, every finite assembly of single $r$-edges and
private expansions of even cycles with a genuine piece--joint
incidence forest satisfies
\[
 t(H,U)\ge p(U)^{|E(H)|}
\]
for every finite normalized probability law and every regular
symmetric kernel $U:\Omega^r\to[0,1]$, without a restriction on
the parent attachment ports.
\end{conjecture}

The core-admissible case and one-joint bouquets of copies of a fixed
cycle expansion are proved above.  Copies meeting at one
common root also give a classical positive case beyond cycles:
identical rooted profiles satisfy $\mathbb E\phi^k\ge(\mathbb E\phi)^k$ by Jensen.
Roots related by an automorphism have the same profile when the
kernel is symmetric.  That identical-profile argument alone does
not cover different port orbits; the mixed-port bouquet result also
uses the core profile bound.  Neither positive case controls all
the correlations at several joints.

A proof of the conjectural direction must control private-parent
messages or supply an appropriate coupling certificate.  A negative
profile covariance is not a counterexample; falsification requires an
actual finite assembly and bounded regular kernel with the full
density strictly below $p^{|E(H)|}$.  The broader question, whether
an obligatory assembly of Sidorenko atoms must itself be Sidorenko,
also remains unresolved by this calculation.  Before describing either
question as globally open or new, its complete geometric class must
be compared with the existing hypergraph reflection criteria.
