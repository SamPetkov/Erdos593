\section{From canonical structure to density: results and questions}

The classification fixes the allowable cyclic pieces, but does not fix
where those pieces are joined.  For density questions this distinction
is consequential.  The following directions develop that distinction
through explicit finite witnesses, a reusable incidence certificate, and
a small list of residual cyclic cores.  Analytical proofs and conjectural
targets are presented separately.  None of the additional statements in
this follow-on note has new Lean validation, and no priority claim is made.

\subsection{Analytical results}

\paragraph{Attachment geometry can reverse density ordering.}
For each $r\ge3$, let $B_r=C_4^{[r]}$ be the private expansion of a four-cycle.
Let $H_{\rm cc}$ and $H_{\rm pp}$ join two copies at core--core and
private--private vertices, respectively.  Both have eight edges,
$8r-9$ vertices, connected Levi cycle rank two, the same two cycle atoms
and the same multiplicity-two joint profile.  Nevertheless neither
dominates the other in density over regular symmetric $r$-kernels.

The new witness uses $Z\in\{1,-1/2\}$ with probabilities $1/3,2/3$ and
\[
 U_r=\frac12+a_r\sum_{i<j}Z_iZ_j
                  -2a_r\sum_{i<j<k}Z_iZ_jZ_k,
 \qquad
 a_r=\frac1{4(\binom r2+2\binom r3)}.
\]
It lies in $[1/4,3/4]$ and is regular.  With
$\varepsilon_r=a_r^4/16$ and $q_r=1/16+\varepsilon_r$, its core and
private rooted profiles are $q_r+\varepsilon_rz$ and
$q_r-2\varepsilon_rz$.  Thus their covariance is
$-\varepsilon_r^2$, while
\[
 t(H_{\rm pp},U_r)-t(H_{\rm cc},U_r)
       =\frac32\varepsilon_r^2>0.
\]
The existing ternary witness in the draft gives the opposite strict
ordering.  This proves incomparability, not a Sidorenko counterexample:
even the mixed-port assembly in the new witness has density
$2^{-8}+\varepsilon_r/8>2^{-8}$.

\paragraph{A genuine geometric sufficient condition.}
In a finite piece--joint incidence forest of single edges and expanded
even cycles, suppose each nonroot cycle meets its parent at a core
vertex of that child cycle.  For every regular symmetric kernel of mean
$p$, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives a rooted bound $p^{2k}$ for a
$2k$-cycle at a core vertex.  Leaf integration then proves
$t(H,U)\ge p^{|E(H)|}$.  Parent-side ports may be private; the condition
concerns the child's upstream port.  One-joint bouquets of copies of
a fixed cycle also satisfy the bound with arbitrary port choices.
These are concrete sufficient conditions built from classical
restricted-density and gluing ideas~\citep{liszegedy2011,szegedy2014},
not a proof of unrestricted assembly closure.

\paragraph{An exposure certificate links structure and sparse hosts.}
Suppose $t\ge2$ ordered classes each contain $N\ge1$ triangular faces
on the same $N+2$ points, and every face after the first introduces a
fresh point.  Their labelled incidence graph is two-vertex-connected
and strictly two-balanced, with
\[
 e=3tN,\quad v=(t+1)N+2,\quad
 \beta=(2t-1)N-1,\quad m_2=\frac{3tN-1}{(t+1)N}.
\]
The proof applies to every vertex-and-edge subgraph, not only induced
ones, and characterizes the equality case.  The case $(t,N)=(2,11)$
recovers the $35$-vertex, $66$-edge incidence core in the recent
counterexample construction~\citep{openai161}.  The general expansion
formula is already Nie--Spiro's Lemma~4.2~\citep{niespiro_expansions2024};
it transfers the certificate, not an automatic density deficit.  In
particular the deletion-scale exponent
$2-(t+1)N/(3tN-1)$ is independent of uniformity.  A polynomial gain
above that scale needs additional positive-excess input~\citep{niespiro2025}.

\paragraph{The next cyclic rank has only four suppressed cores.}
A finite simple two-connected bipartite graph of cycle rank three
suppresses to four parallel edges, a triangle with multiplicities
$(2,2,1)$, an alternating doubled four-cycle, or $K_4$.
Path lengths must respect branch colours, and parallel length-one
paths are excluded by original simplicity.  The first family is
covered by the known even and odd generalized-theta
inequalities~\citep{imliliu2024}.  The remaining universal cases are
not settled by this checkpoint.  This is a structural localization,
not an improved proved density cutoff.

\subsection{Equally visible conjectural directions}

\paragraph{Rank three: a graph question and an atom question.}
For every simple two-connected bipartite $J$ of cycle rank three, is
$t(J,W)\ge(\mathbb EW)^{|E(J)|}$ for every finite symmetric graph kernel
$W\in[0,1]$?  For the atom problem, it would suffice to prove this only
for $W=M_U$, the pair marginal of an actual symmetric $r$-kernel,
$r\ge3$.  The stronger graph question implies the weaker atom question;
the converse is not automatic.  The four-core list gives an explicit
finite set of shape families, but arbitrary permitted path lengths and
parity patterns still require an argument.  Falsification needs an
exact full density deficit, not a failed proof or a floating-point sign.

Marginal feasibility is itself a useful boundary.  On a positive-weight
finite carrier, a zero-diagonal $M$ is a symmetric nonnegative $r$-kernel
marginal, with no prescribed upper bound, exactly when the weighted pairs
$\mu(x)\mu(y)M(x,y)$ admit a nonnegative fractional $K_r$ decomposition.
Requiring $U\le1$ adds the capacities
$w_S\le(r-2)!\prod_{z\in S}\mu(z)$.  The supporting proof and four-point diamond
obstruction are in the companion fragment.  Fractional decompositions
are classical~\citep{dross2016}; this criterion is not presented as a
new general theory or as the universal matching-polytope theorem
already in the draft.

\paragraph{Attachment closure without a core-port orientation.}
Does every finite forest assembly of single $r$-edges and expanded
even cycles satisfy the density lower bound for every regular
symmetric $r$-kernel, regardless of its attachment ports?  The
core-admissible and one-joint replica cases above are positive evidence.
The negative-covariance example prevents one tempting proof, but does
not refute the claim.  A proof must control private-port or multi-port
messages; a counterexample must give a genuine assembly whose full
density is below $p^{|E(H)|}$.  Existing coupling and reflection
criteria must be reconciled with the entire geometric class before
this is described as a globally new or open conjecture.

\paragraph{Quantitative witnesses rather than fixed-uniformity existence.}
For the fixed $35$-vertex incidence core $J$, put $A_r=J^{[r]}$.
A working target is to find constants $c,C>0,r_0$ such that every
$r\ge r_0$ has a finite uniform carrier of size at most $r^C$ and a
regular symmetric $[0,1]$-kernel of mean $p_r\ge r^{-C}$ with
\[
 t(A_r,U_r)\le(1-cr^{-66})p_r^{66}.
\]
This asks to combine two advantages not combined by the present routes.
Conditionally on the full quantitative family, the matching lift gives
the $r^{-66}$ relative gap but only $p_r\ge r^{-Cr}$; a simpler pair
average gives polynomial mean but the weaker $r^{-132}$ gap.
The primary construction already
contains quantitative rank-layer estimates.  What remains to be checked
is their extraction into a complete proper-subgraph-flat family with
exact regularity and a polynomial-size uniform carrier.  Qualitative
convergence alone would not supply this combined contract.
That extraction is a source-validation task, not advertised as a new
open problem.  Combining polynomial mean and the larger gap is the
separate working conjectural direction; neither its truth nor its
originality has been established here.

These questions are research directions, not additions to the count of
proved results.  Their common subject is what cyclic structure permits,
what attachment geometry changes, and which density information survives
the passage to higher uniformity.  The main paper should retain the
strongest audited endpoints of this chain; detailed certificates and
formalization mechanics can remain in supporting material.
