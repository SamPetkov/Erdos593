\subsection{What would make the extended paper substantive?}

There is no defensible promise that this paper will exceed the ``least
valuable'' member of OpenAI's mathematical release.  Mathematical value is
not a single ordered quantity, and the release contains results at different
stages of verification.  Its own README warns that some unformalized outputs
may have issues.  A useful benchmark is instead an exact additional theorem,
a sharp boundary or obstruction, a reusable method, and a faithful account
of the proof and its dependencies.

The comparison should also start from the correct chronology.  Li's preprint,
first submitted on 23 June 2026, already states the triple-system
classification and exact uncountable avoidance spectrum.  Our current
draft acknowledges this priority and the independent development of its
proof.  The added mathematical case must therefore rest on genuinely
additional structural or density results, not on presenting the same
classification as a first solution.

Three primary-source benchmarks illustrate distinct contribution shapes.
The Sidorenko manuscript specifies a $35$-vertex, $66$-edge bipartite
counterexample and a finite simple witnessing host.  Its documented Lean
endpoint excludes the separate forcing consequence.  The van der Waerden
manuscript states one absolute threshold for
$W_r(k)>k^{10^{-5}k\lfloor\log_2r\rfloor}$ for every $r\ge2$;
the explicit uniform quantifiers are part of the claim.  The threshold
decomposition manuscript obtains bounded fixed pieces from a major
companion input, but expressly does not require their separate embeddings
to agree on shared vertices.  These are source-described contributions,
not independent correctness verdicts or a ranking of the collection.

For our paper, the strongest potential additions are not the elementary
finite-sum contraction or the private-expansion density transformation
already proved by Nie--Spiro.  Subject to full proof and prior-art audits,
the current draft's universal marginal-realization theorem, sharp
uniformity and girth-dependent estimates, higher-uniformity structural
classification, and exact fixed-atom assembly geometry are stronger
candidates for the main contribution.  A conditional consequence of the
external rank-layer construction must retain that condition, even when
the resulting all-uniformity conclusion is useful.

The three follow-on directions fit this argument as boundaries and tests.
Low-rank recognition identifies a positive single-atom regime without
settling the next rank or general assembly closure.  Incidence
balancedness supplies an explicit certificate rather than a new general
density-transformation formula.  Full port profiles expose the information
needed to combine atoms; the contraction identity alone supplies neither
an inequality nor a positive correlation sign.  A proved, checkable
compatibility criterion or a genuine obstruction would add more than
several further formal restatements of the identity.

The conjectural directions should receive equally visible treatment, as
the author requested.  Each needs exact quantifiers, a relation to existing
results, boundary cases, evidence, and a concrete route to falsification.
They remain conjectures: equal exposition cannot replace a proof of the
principal theorem.  The new Lean candidates also remain uncompiled after
the two recorded precompiler runner failures.  A source-reviewed argument,
a formal statement and a successfully audited Lean export are different
deliverables.

The coherent story is the distinction between forcing and density.
Uncountable chromatic number selects allowable canonical atoms; finite
density depends on their cyclic content and on compatible attachment
profiles.  Exact assembly geometry then describes what can vary while
the atoms remain fixed.  One complete new theorem along this chain can
support a substantive research paper.  The present checkpoint does not
yet certify that result, whole-paper formalization, novelty, or a journal
outcome.
