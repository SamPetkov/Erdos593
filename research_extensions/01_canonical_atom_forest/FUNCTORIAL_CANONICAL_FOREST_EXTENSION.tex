\begin{proposition}[Functorial canonical atom forest]
\label{proposition-functorial-canonical-atom-forest}
Let $F$ be a finite reduced obligatory triple system.  Let $\mathcal A(F)$
be its canonical atoms and let $S(F)$ be the set of points belonging to at
least two atoms.  Form the bipartite incidence forest $Q(F)$ on
$\mathcal A(F)\sqcup S(F)$.

Every isomorphism $\varphi:F\to G$ induces a unique forest isomorphism
$Q(\varphi):Q(F)\to Q(G)$, with
\[
Q(\varphi)(A)=\varphi(A),\qquad Q(\varphi)(p)=\varphi(p).
\]
These induced maps respect identities and composition.

Moreover, $F$ is recovered from the disjoint union of its atoms by identifying
exactly the copies of each shared point prescribed by $Q(F)$.  If $F$ is
presented as any forest assembly of connected one-point-indecomposable
obligatory systems whose hyperedge sets partition $E(F)$, then those pieces
are precisely the canonical atoms and the resulting piece--point incidence
forest is $Q(F)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An isomorphism of triple systems induces a bipartition-preserving isomorphism
of Levi graphs.  Bridges and cyclic blocks are invariant under graph
isomorphism, so the intrinsic construction of the canonical atom partition is
carried to the corresponding partition of $G$.  Shared points and atom--point
incidence are therefore preserved, proving functoriality.

For reconstruction, distinct atoms meet in at most one point and every point
appearing in more than one atom is represented by its unique shared-point node
of $Q(F)$.  Thus the indicated quotient makes exactly the identifications
already present in $F$ and no others.

Finally, each one-point-indecomposable obligatory factor is, by
Corollary~\ref{corollary-minimal-generators-indecomposables}, either one triple
or $J^+$ for a finite $2$-connected simple bipartite graph $J$.  A simple Levi
cycle cannot cross a one-point amalgamation, so cyclic Levi blocks remain
inside the proposed factors, while a single-triple factor remains an
all-bridge hyperedge.  Hence the canonical atom extraction recovers exactly
those factors, and the shared-point forest is forced.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Decorated-forest isomorphism criterion]
\label{corollary-decorated-forest-isomorphism}
Two finite reduced obligatory triple systems $F$ and $G$ are isomorphic if and
only if there is a bipartite forest isomorphism $\theta:Q(F)\to Q(G)$ together
with, for every atom $A$ of $F$, an isomorphism
\[
f_A:A\longrightarrow\theta(A)
\]
that sends every shared point $p\in V(A)$ to $\theta(p)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Necessity follows from the proposition.  Conversely, the local maps agree on
every overlap because overlaps are precisely the shared points and their
images are prescribed by $\theta$.  They therefore glue through the canonical
reconstruction to a global triple-system isomorphism.
\end{proof}
